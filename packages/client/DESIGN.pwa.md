# Green Goods Client PWA: Design Brief

> Installed PWA creative direction for the gardener-facing field tool. Use with the root `DESIGN.md`; lint this overlay and the root file separately.

## Surface Identity

| Mode | Detection | Audience | Metaphor | Paradigm | Navigation |
|------|-----------|----------|----------|----------|------------|
| **Installed PWA** | `display-mode: standalone` | Gardeners | Working in the garden | Command Surface | Bottom `AppBar`: Home, Garden, Profile |

**Hard rule:** Installed PWA = app. Use bottom `AppBar`; never show the browser `SiteHeader` or hamburger chrome.

**Routing:** presentation-mode loaders gate browser vs PWA entry. Installed/authenticated PWA routes live under `/home` and render through `PwaRuntime` + `AppShell` with the bottom `AppBar`.

## The Field Tool

**Physical metaphor:** A gardener's well-worn journal. Practical, personal, tactile.

**Layout philosophy:**
- Mobile-first, thumb-zone optimized
- AppBar with 3 tabs: Home (with its count badge), Garden, Profile. The active tab shows a green pill behind its icon, the cockpit's recipe (DL-107): `--primary-container` behind the line icon in `--primary-container-foreground`, and the label in ink at weight 600. Every tab holds the pill's 56px, so no tab moves when the active one changes, and the bar stays solid white and 69px tall
- AppBar hides for immersive surfaces: Submit Work (`/home/garden/*`), a garden's own page and everything under it (`/home/:id/**`: the garden banner carries back, and work detail, promise detail, composer and proof routes carry their own fixed action bar exactly where the nav would sit), and whenever any sheet or dialog is open (DL-015). `isImmersiveAppRoute` in `config/pwaRouting.ts` owns the route rule. Sheet primitives register themselves while open, so there is no list of sheets to keep in sync
- Sheet heights (DL-014): every bottom sheet names one tier and never sets its own height. `compact` sizes to its content up to 50% (confirmations, resume prompts, gardener and badge details, a pool's agreement, Offer or Request); `half` holds 50% (short choices and a single preview: take a promise up, the profile photo, season and campaign details, the promise from Submit Work); `tall` holds 70% (lists and short forms: notifications, link work, request to join, withdraw an offer, conviction, the promise mid-proof); `full` holds 85% (tabbed workspaces, long reviews, and the garden filters: wallet, promises, your work, endowment, filter gardens, vault checkout, confirm a promise). Photo viewers and public records open full screen. The steward feedback drawer on a work page is not a sheet but caps at the half value and renders the shared header. No other height exists: the sheet surface sets no cap of its own and the centered dialogs share one 90vh cap
- Sheet header (DL-028): every sheet and dialog renders the one shared header (`SheetHeader`, through `PwaSheet`, `AppSheet`, the work dashboard, and the centered `DialogShell` and `ConfirmDialog`): a 40×6 grip that drags to dismiss on every bottom sheet, the title at 18px semibold on a 24px line, an optional target line under the title that names what a consequential act writes to, an optional description at 14px on a 20px line, both wrapping rather than clipping, no leading icon (a confirmation's tone lives in its filled action and its alertdialog role), a 44px borderless Close button labeled "Close" on the title's line, and no rule under the header; a hairline appears only while the body scrolls under it, and a tab rail sits directly under the header with its own rule. The title labels the dialog. The cockpit's dialogs take the same sheet material, grip and header below 640px at their own 22px title, and their grip does not drag (DL-104). Below the header, every heading in the body (section headings, group labels over lists, and success state titles) uses `SheetHeading`: 14px semibold on a 20px line in the strong text color, in sentence case, never small capitals. The client stylesheet carries no element type rules (`h1`–`h6`, `p`), so the shared header renders as declared; the legacy client type utilities (`title-section`, `body-sm-regular`) are gone with it
- Sheet tab states (DL-035, DL-036, DL-106): loading, error, empty, and not-ready states are the shared state block (`EmptyState`: a 56px round tile, an 18px title on `type-title-md`, one line, and the act), and they live in the available content region after the tab's own status or filter row. Empty-state icons and titles use a shared upper-middle anchor; descriptions and actions grow below it without shifting that anchor. The Work dashboard, Wallet, Promises, and Endowment sheets share this vertical anatomy, so switching tabs never moves an equivalent state into a different zone
- List loading (DL-056): a list under a header row holds its loaded layout while it loads. The header row keeps its height with "Gathering…" where the count lands (screen readers hear what is gathering) and placeholders the width of the loaded filters, and skeleton rows take the loaded row's own frame, so nothing moves when the data arrives. A failed read keeps the header row's space and takes the empty state's anatomy and anchor: an error icon, a title, one line saying nothing was lost, and Try Again. The garden's Promises and Work tabs work this way; neither shows a centered spinner
- Sheet motion (DL-033): a bottom sheet moves like a native one. It slides up in one move on `--spring-spatial-slow` and never rises above its resting edge, so the page never shows under it: no overshoot, no surface fade, and an upward drag stops at rest. A drag from the grip or the header's title block under it moves the sheet one to one while the scrim lifts with it; the grip stays the visible affordance and Close stays outside the gesture. Letting go closes the sheet after a downward flick or past a quarter of its height, and otherwise settles it back on `--spring-spatial`; an upward flick always keeps it open. A tap on the dimmed backdrop closes it like Escape and Close, and `preventClose` holds every path during in-flight work. A close from rest accelerates out on `--spring-spatial-exit`; a flicked sheet already has speed and leaves on `--spring-spatial`. The gesture writes `translate` and the keyframes own `transform`, because a finished keyframe animation outranks inline styles; a sheet never sets its own motion
- Filter Gardens is a `full` sheet: membership scope and sort each use one full-width choice per row; domains use two equal-width choices per row, with an odd final choice spanning the full row. Sorting offers Name (A-Z) or Newest first; the sheet opens on and resets to Newest first (DL-040). The body scrolls above the pinned Reset Filters bar when more options or longer labels need room (DL-037); Profile Photo is a `half` sheet with four fixed regions (title-only header, an 80px preview of the current photo, the fallback, or the unpublished draft, a two-line status slot that shows the privacy notice by default, and at most two bar actions), the draft's discard control beside its pill, and removal confirmed through the shared confirmation stacked on top
- Sheet actions (DL-016): every sheet and dialog pins its actions in the shared action bar (`SheetActions`, through the `actions` prop of `PwaSheet`, `DialogShell`, `ConfirmDialog`, and `AppSheet`), under a body that scrolls above it; no sheet renders its own action buttons. Below 640px the actions stack full width with the primary on top: a filled primary (the error fill when destructive), then an outlined second action, Cancel included, and a text action only for a rare third choice. Step navigation (Back / Continue, as in wallet send) keeps one row. From 640px the same bar is one right-aligned row with the primary rightmost. Labels wrap rather than cut off, and a loading action stays focusable. Page-level bars (Submit Work, work approval, promise detail, the work page) are not sheets and keep their own layout
- Work page (DL-057): unsent, blocked, or checking work says why in a notice under its title, with the icon and title on one line and the reason in two lines at most from 320 to 390px in every language. The fixed bar holds only the acts: two equal 48px halves on one row, Discard (secondary, danger tone) left and the primary right, or one full-width act while the work may already be sent. The promise page's queued and failed notices share the anatomy, with a two-line minimum so every variant has one height and their actions full width below
- Step flows (DL-058): each step is named under its marker (Submit Work: Start, Media, Details, Review; adding proof: Media, Details, Review). Once a promise is chosen, every step opens with its FormInfo heading card and then the pinned promise, the shared commitment row with "Work for" or "Proof for" on its detail line (DL-110), which sticks under the top bar (72px below the status bar's safe area) as the page scrolls. Tapping it opens the promise in a sheet (Submit Work's offers Not for This Promise), and closing the sheet returns to the same step
- Toasts (DL-060): the status icon sits on the title's line (on the message's first line when there is no title), and the message, description, and action run the toast's full width below it; the action's text takes `--primary-on-surface`. The toast is shared, so the admin cockpit renders the same anatomy
- Garden header (DL-020): page actions (notifications, endowment, share) are 32px icon buttons stacked in the banner's top right, with Share last so the others keep their places. The title row carries at most one text action, Join Garden or Request to Join, at the `compact` button size (32px tall, 14px label, no icon, 48px tap area), and its location and founded lines truncate instead of running under the button
- Logo screens (DL-084): every screen that draws the logo shares one geometry, so nothing moves between them: sign-in in all its states, the loading screen, and the screen shown when the app could not open. `SplashScaffold` owns it: the logo (96px, 112px from 640px), one line under it, two 44px control slots, the message zone and the text link, with the free space split 11:9 above and below and extra space reserved above to lower the block another 4px when space permits. This places the block 24px below the previous anchor. The line is the app's name in the wordmark's voice (20px bold, green) or a sentence in the status voice (16px semibold, ink: "Signing you in...", "Green Goods is loading."), centered in the same slot and one line long from 320px in every language. A button on a logo screen is full width with the app's corner in sign-in's first slot; anything longer than the line becomes a quiet note under the button, never a second line above it. The loading screen is static HTML that paints before React (`.boot-pwa-shell` in `index.html`) and mirrors the scaffold slot for slot; `bootFallbackGeometry.test.ts` fails when either side changes alone
- Failure states (DL-085): a failure is drawn where it happened, and only as large as what failed. One screen failing keeps the shell: the state block's anatomy (DL-056, DL-106) in place of the screen, with the bottom bar where the route shows it. It has the error mark, a title, one line saying nothing was lost, a full-width Reload at sign-in's width, and an outlined Back on routes that hide the bar (`AppScreenError`). The app itself failing (it could not open, or its shell failed) is the logo screen above: one line, Reload in sign-in's first slot, and a quiet note (`AppLaunchError`), in the language the app would have chosen. Offline is a wait, not a fault: the calm mark, a line saying the screen loads when the connection returns, and it does, by itself. A screen whose code is from an older build reloads itself once and shows no error. The state belongs to the screen that failed: moving to another screen clears it, including inside a garden, whose page stays mounted across its child pages. No screen shows an error message, a stack, a copy-details action or a restart that clears data: the report is kept on the device and sent to PostHog as soon as it can be. `ErrorRecovery` owns the behavior; `RouteErrorBoundary` and `AppErrorBoundary` only place it
- Scrolling: content scrolls inside `#app-scroll`, within a full-height `main` that clips and is positioned, so the document itself never scrolls. Android stretches every fixed element when the document overscrolls; with no document scroll, the AppBar and page headers hold still while native pull-to-refresh still reaches the document
- Persistent chrome (AppBar, offline banner) has its own view-transition name and never animates, so tab cross-fades pass beneath it
- Safe areas: the installed iPhone app has a solid status bar (`apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style: default`), so iOS places the app below it and the page reaches the bottom edge; it still draws under the home indicator. iOS keeps the style an app was added with, so older installs keep the see-through bar and draw under the status bar too, as Android's edge-to-edge mode does. The controls of the top bar (`TopNav`, 72px below the status bar's safe area), page headers and the garden banner start 16px below that safe area (`--app-top-offset`); the offline banner is 16px tall and shows in that space, so it never covers a control. In a mobile browser tab that isn't installed, where the install prompt can show, the space is 24px: the prompt stays 24px tall so its Profile and Dismiss buttons stay usable, and it marks the root (`data-install-prompt`) from the first paint until the tab closes, so dismissing it moves nothing. Toasts and the photo viewer's controls also start below `env(safe-area-inset-top)`, and pages and the pinned promise card under the top bar start at `--app-top-bar-height`. Bottom bars, sheets and the space kept clear of them pad by `--safe-area-bottom`, never by `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` directly (`bun run check --only design-tokens` fails on a raw bottom inset in client or PWA sheet source). On iOS 26 an install with the see-through bar is laid out a status bar short of the screen while the page is shorter than the screen, so in the installed app the page is at least as tall as the screen (the standalone `html` min-height in `index.css`): iOS then gives it the whole screen, and older installs' bars reach the bottom edge after an update, with no reinstall. If iOS keeps the short layout anyway, it fills the band below with the page background, and the token drops the part of the inset the band already covers. Both insets are 0 on Android, so nothing moves there

**Typography:**
- Plus Jakarta Sans only, the cockpit's face, so the app and the cockpit read as one product (DL-096); no serif. This is a tool, not a magazine. `index.html` loads the variable face and the loading screen names it; a public page opened inside the installed app keeps the website's Inter.
- Type comes from the shared theme tokens and the shared components' own rules. The client's legacy type utilities (`.title-screen`, `.label-md`, `.body-md-regular`, and siblings) read tokens no stylesheet defines and are being retired; `.title-section` and `.body-sm-regular` are already gone (DL-028). The client stylesheet sets no element type sizes, so every heading names its own size and weight (`typographyLayer.guard.test.ts` fails a heading that does not)
- Type roles at the app's density (root `DESIGN.md` § Typography): `type-body` 16px for reading; `type-label` 16px and `type-label-sm` 14px for labels, field labels, and secondary lines; `type-meta` 12px for metadata; `type-meta-sm` 11px for chips and status pills; titles at 24, 18, and 16px. The shared components use them, and the app's own views still use the shared scale (`text-label-md` and its siblings) until they move

**Content hierarchy:**
1. Active garden context: always visible, always grounding
2. Work in progress: drafts, submissions, the thing you're doing now
3. Garden activity: what others are contributing
4. Your impact: personal contribution history

**Words:** the app calls commitments promises: the garden's Promises tab, Home's Promises launcher and sheet, and every string that names them ("Promesas", "Promessas"). Offers and requests are both promises; "pool" names only the container (its agreement, a paused or closed pool). Code identifiers, routes, the ontology, and the docs keep "commitment" (DL-059).

**Offline behavior:**
- Warm, reassuring offline indicators, not error-red
- Your Work has two tabs, Pending and Completed. Pending is one list sorted by what needs you: can't upload, work to review (stewards), to upload, drafts, checking, then sent work in review; unsent proof lists beside work. Home's Your Work badge counts what is still on this phone (drafts, work and reviews to upload or being checked, unsent proof), not sent work in review, and Upload all sends what is ready without a count (DL-061)
- Draft persistence is invisible (you never lose work), and a work draft keeps the promise it is for: resuming it brings the promise back into Submit Work, checked against the live promises (DL-062)

**Window Controls Overlay:** CSS is ready for desktop PWA titlebar integration (`.app-titlebar` with `app-region: drag`). Currently prepared but not active in components.

## Color Adaptation

The PWA inherits the Warm Earth core and the cockpit's ground (DL-097):
- White cards on the linen canvas (`bg-canvas`), with a `neutral-200` card hairline so a card still reads on linen. In dark the canvas is the Earth ramp's warm floor and a card sits ten points of lightness above it, so a card never blends into the ground.
- A bar that covers scrolled content paints the canvas: the top bar, the garden header and its tab row, the pinned promise and the Profile header. The tab row and the garden title block take the ground they sit on, so a tab row inside a sheet takes the sheet's surface.
- The browser's status bar and the install manifest take the app's ground: linen, and the warm floor in dark. A browser tab keeps the website's own ground: white, and near-black in dark.
- Fields, chips and secondary buttons draw the control outline, neutral-500 (neutral-400 in dark). Focus is one solid 2px ring in the app's green, mint in dark, and button labels are weight 500, the cockpit's (DL-098).

Green accents follow one theme-aware rhythm (DL-086, DL-088; PRD-1150):
- Green labels, icons, active navigation marks, indicator bars, selection borders, switches, radio buttons and checkboxes use `--primary-on-surface`: deep green in light and pale mint `tertiary-dark` (`#C2F5DA`) in dark.
- Any green fill that carries text, a number, or a glyph (filled CTAs, count badges, step markers, selected chips, pills) uses the contrast-safe action pair: deep green with white content in light, mint with near-black `on-tertiary-dark` ink in dark. Dark hover uses `tertiary-dark-hover` (`#84EBB4`).
- Every count is the shared `CountBadge` (DL-099): a capsule 16px tall and at least 16px wide, with 11px numerals at weight 600 on that action pair, wherever the app counts: the AppBar's Home tab, Home's launchers and filter, a garden's notification bell, and every tab row. On an icon or a control's edge it draws a 2px ring of the surface behind it, the canvas or the AppBar's white; beside a label it draws none. It reads 99+ above 99, and 9+ on the AppBar.
- Active tab labels, their icons and indicators, links, selected titles, and media seek controls use the same role. The AppBar's active tab is the exception: its icon sits on a pill of the green container, deep green on green-100 in light and pale green on green-900 in dark, and its label is ink (DL-107). Audio play/pause uses the theme's filled-action pair.
- Soft green highlights may retain the tertiary tint. Domain and status colors stay independent; success, warning, error and information marks are not brand accents.
- A red fill that carries white text (the filled danger button, error badges) uses `--error-action`, red-700 in both themes (DL-053).
- A blue fill that carries white text (the information badge, the current submission stage) uses `--information-action`, blue-500 in every theme, because the dark app lifts the base blue for its icons (DL-097).
- Shared runtime aliases scope the mint palette to PWA presentation and Storybook app surfaces; public and admin retain their own palettes. The static boot fallback mirrors the dark action pair before styles load.

**Dynamic garden theming** (future): Each garden can tint the experience with accent color derived from its banner image. The foundation palette stays, and only the accent shifts.

## Forms and Lists

- Buttons and fields come only from the shared primitives: `Button`, `IconButton`, and `Chip` for actions, and `TextInput`, `Textarea`, `NativeSelect`, `Switch`, `FormattedAmountInput`, `DatePicker`, and `FileUploadField` for fields (DL-025, DL-031); the switch carries a 44 px hit box. Every button takes the app's 16px corner, the same corner as the field beside it, whatever its emphasis (DL-026, DL-029), tightening to 12px while pressed; a text action has no container until hover, an icon-only button is a circle, and a chip is a capsule. Fields are 16px (DL-022). Buttons and fields share one height scale (DL-023): 48 px for page-level primaries (Upload Work, work approval), 44 px by default and in sheets, 40 px in dense rows, and 32 px `compact` beside text or in a header, always with a 48 px tap area.
- Every form field reserves two lines (32 px at 12/16 px type) under its control for a hint or error, and fields in a form sit 8 px apart. `FormField`, `FormattedAmountInput` and `DatePicker` hold the reserve with the shared message line (`gg-field-message`); `FileUploadField` still puts its help above the upload well. A longer message grows the slot instead of scrolling inside it (DL-018). The cockpit's fields take the same label and message layout at their own corner (DL-105).
- A list header that pairs a status line with filters stays one row at phone width. The status line and its compact Refresh keep their width; the filters give way first, as `NativeSelect density="condensed"` sized to the chosen option with a 64 px floor, ending in an ellipsis while the open picker still shows every option in full (DL-032). Header filters take `controlSize="compact"`: a 32 px face, the height of the compact buttons beside them, inside transparent borders that keep a 48 px tap area (DL-055). Your Work, the garden's Promises tab, and its Work tab use them.
- Every record in a list is the shared item row (`ItemRow`, DL-102; a decision row keeps its two paired acts), the app's card on its 16px corner: 88 px in every state and language, the item's own photo (square and as tall as the row, its photo count on it) or a centered 56 px icon tile, a one-line 15 px title with the full title on hover, the one status pill under it, one 11 px detail line, and the row's one action in its right slot, or, on a row with none, the amount the item carries (DL-113). The detail line holds at most two details in the type's order and never wraps: a detail that does not fit drops whole, last first, and a first detail too long on its own ends in an ellipsis. Ages take their short form and are always a number ("2 hr. ago", "hace 2 h", "há 2 h"). The row owns the size, so the photo and the row change height together (DL-034).
- Work rows add the 2 px status edge and give who, then how long ago, with feedback in the age's place when the work has some; on Your Work's Completed rows the first detail says whose work it was. Every Your Work › Pending row, proof included, takes the same row: the kind's pill under the title, where it stands and then when on its detail line (a status that only repeats the pill drops), the hand-heart mark on the photo for proof and for work that keeps a promise, the guarded Discard in the right slot, and a status edge; a blocked row's pill says what can't happen and its line why, in two or three words (DL-061). Card lists keep 12 px between cards (`--gg-list-gap`).
- Promises are the shared commitment row (DL-110) on the Promises sheet, a garden's Promises tab and a group's page: the offer's seedling or the request's helping hand leads in the tile, named for screen readers, and copies made together are one row with their number on the tile and what is still available in the pill. The pill is the promise's state, or "Needs you", or while it lasts a queue state ("Confirming…", "Not sent", "Didn't send"), and the 2px edge follows it. The details give the due date, then the other person from the reader's side ("due 24 Oct · maria.eth"); a queue state's reason, or what kind of ask a To confirm row is, leads instead. A promise still on the phone has no page yet, so its units come first and its reason follows ("6 hours · Saved on this phone…"), and it keeps Discard and Send in its right slot. How the reader is involved, the units and the team are on the promise, and its queue notice stays at the top of the promise's page.
- With DL-111's code change, Submit Work picks its action and its garden from the shared pickers (DL-111): media cards with a 104px banner in one row that scrolls sideways where the carousel stood, each card 90% of the row and snapping to its start. Each picker is one horizontal listbox (`aria-orientation="horizontal"`), named for what it picks ("Select Your Action", "Select Your Garden"), with one tab stop, where each card exposes whether it is chosen (`aria-selected`), the arrow keys move between cards and wrap, Home and End go to the first and last card, and Space, Enter or a press chooses (moving never chooses), and the chosen card takes the focus role's 3px border in the app's green. An action card shows its domain on the banner (an action whose domain the app does not know, listed as Other, takes a neutral banner with no mark) and what the action asks for ("2 photos required"); a garden card, here, on Home's list and on a work's page, shows the garden's name, then how many members and where. Assessments on a garden's Insights tab are item rows: the list icon, the title, the reporting period (the day it was recorded when it covers none) and the domain, the anatomy the cockpit's assessment rows share; the app lists no hypercerts.
- A list of people (a garden's members, the people you can send to) is the shared person row (`PersonRow`, DL-112): the item row led by the person's 56 px round avatar, their photo or the app's default avatar, their name as the shared address display gives it (their Green Goods name, then their ENS name, then a name the list holds for them, such as a member's username, then a short address), their highest role as the row's one pill, and one detail: since when a member has been a gardener ("Gardener since"), or the garden that tells two people you could send to apart. The indexer records one date per gardener, at their first gardener role in any garden, so the row never says when they joined this garden. The recipient you chose wears the open row's border but reads as pressed, since choosing someone is not opening a record. A promise's team keeps its own rows, which carry each person's assigned rows and credits.
- A garden's cookie jar, a vault in its endowment summary and the reader's deposit are item rows with no action on the row (DL-113): the asset, one pill (a jar's Active or Paused; a too-low limit is a notice on the jar's page, since only a steward can raise it), one detail (what a claim pays, how many have deposited, or the reader's shares) and the amount on the right, its value with the asset, never cut, over what it is. The wallet, where a gardener claims, shows what can be taken now; a garden's endowment sheet shows what the jar holds. A jar's or a deposit's row opens its page in place, right under it on the card surface at the row's corner: the limit with its cadence, the holdings and the claim form, or the withdrawal. A vault summary row is content: a deposit starts from the sheet's Deposit section and its pinned Deposit, as before. The wallet's and the endowment sheet's jars are one row (`CookieJarRow`) with one claim form.
- The facts about one thing, a review before a send, a record's details or the Profile's settings, are the shared fact row (`FactRow` in a `FactList`, DL-109): a label beside its value in divided rows, the label at 14px in the sub ink and the value at 16px, wrapping rather than cut, on the card surface or inset in the sheet section that frames it. A row's one action takes its right slot, as the send review's Change does. A setting is a row with no value whose control takes the slot; in the Profile's settings each title stays on one line and each hint reserves two, so a status change never moves a row. A screen that marks each fact with a glyph, as the promise pages do, keeps it before the label.
- A status pill on a row or card (work and Pending rows, promise rows, season and campaign cards) is `StatusBadge size="xs"`: an 11 px label on a 22 px capsule, a card's only pill. A page's own status block keeps `sm` (DL-054).

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Test PWA layouts at 375px and on a real phone when layout or navigation changes
- Preserve the theme-aware green hierarchy in `/home`, `/home/garden`, and `/home/profile`
- Use container queries for components that can appear in both client modes
- Keep copy warm, personal, and community-facing

**Don't:**
- Show `SiteHeader` or hamburger navigation in installed PWA mode
- Use editorial serif in the PWA (it is an app, not a magazine)
- Replace the PWA green roles with admin workspace colors
- Make the funding or submission flow feel transactional
