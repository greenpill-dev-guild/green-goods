# Pilot specification

**Last updated:** 2026-10-15. US/EU donors are the initial priority; later regional routes and their evidence are owned by [regions.md](regions.md). The existing hub slug is retained so earlier references remain valid. This is still a backlog plan, not an implemented or approved checkout.

## Intended donor experience

**First time:** choose the garden and local-currency amount; enter email and authenticate with a code; complete the selected provider's account/verification requirements; select the permitted payment method; review total cost, timing and ownership terms; press one final **Pay and endow** button. Complete any issuer authentication or bank-app payment. The app delivers the purchased asset to the donor wallet, executes the already-authorized vault deposit, then confirms the endowment and emails a receipt. No required passkey, wallet selector, network selection, gas purchase, swap screen or second deposit button.

Email-first does not mean email-only. Provider account consent, phone codes, identity checks and 3DS remain visible and counted. The US cohort targets L0; the EU cohort follows its own required verification. Use the [regional verification profiles](regions.md#provider-decisions), not a global no-ID claim. A thirdweb wallet and a payment-provider account are separate identities; matching email does not establish shared authentication or shared KYC.

**Returning:** recover the same thirdweb wallet through email when needed; retrieve the consented provider customer and permitted saved payment details; recheck eligibility, limits and quote; show current fees and press the same final button. Reuse verified details where permitted. Provider reauthentication, risk checks and bank authentication may still occur. Do not silently create a replacement wallet or send a donor to another provider after an uncertain payment.

**No second confirmation** means no post-payment Green Goods deposit/signature/“finish endowment” action. It does not mean one screen or no bank approval. The primary local-payment routes must also avoid an additional “I have paid” report or manual agent confirmation; current manual Fonbnk routes fail that checkout-quality gate. Preserve required provider disclosures and clear crypto/endowment consent rather than hiding them to reduce the click count.

The final review says the donor is buying crypto to fund a redeemable endowment, identifies the garden and principal/withdrawal rules, and explicitly authorizes the subsequent deposit. The app abstracts technical choices while preserving that consent. A charitable tax receipt is not assumed for an endowment.

## Provider and asset prerequisites

Before live work, the organisation must arrange Stripe Embedded Components Onramp approval, provisioned Link OAuth credentials and the applicable preview API version for each US/EU cohort. Obtain written permission for this exact sequence: purchase into a donor-owned wallet followed by an automatically authorized garden endowment. Confirm states/countries, minimums, actual credit-card rules, support/refunds and receiving-organisation requirements. Provider availability in the EU does not establish an EU-approved DAI conversion or endowment strategy: the complete downstream asset/use case needs approval. No provider contact is authorized by this planning request.

Later regions need their own provider onboarding, allowed origins, signed server credentials, webhook verification and country/payment permissions. Reuse the Green Goods funding-intent and ownership boundaries; add a narrow adapter only after selecting that route. Do not create all three integrations in parallel or treat a generic provider country list as commercial/legal approval. Resolve provider-specific verification conflicts before enrollment.

For each provider, prove that its supported integration can bind the donor wallet, hide technical selectors, expose the final quote and record bounded endowment consent before payment. A branded widget alone does not prove this. Count provider review/payment controls; never claim the proposed single checkout is already supplied by Ramp or Fonbnk. If the provider cannot support the required consent/execution sequence, reject that integration shape before live funding.

thirdweb needs the existing stable client identity, allowed origins, an approved production wallet/execution configuration, and funded sponsorship/billing. Keep the same client identity across recovery; thirdweb describes [app-scoped email wallets and key export](https://portal.thirdweb.com/wallets/security). Never use an organisation/server wallet as the Stripe delivery address or as the donor's share owner.

Resolve an existing Arbitrum vault's deployed code, `asset()`, decimals, deposit and redemption rules, share receiver, strategy and garden yield beneficiary before selecting the route. A garden treasury, signal pool or general protocol deployment is not automatically a donor-owned endowment vault.

Stripe's documented entry asset here is **native USDC on Arbitrum**, not bridged USDC.e and not a verified ETH/DAI onramp. Native USDC is the common candidate for the other routes; verify the exact token contract, chain and quote for each country/provider. If the selected vault requires DAI, ETH or WETH, prove one approved same-chain conversion and exact approval/deposit batch. Bind minimum output, maximum input, fees and share receiver. Do not use a bridge or silently change the vault asset. No new vault, strategy or settlement contract is within this small pilot; their necessity is a stop-and-rescope result.

## Automatic execution and ownership

Start the technical spike with a **single-use, donor-signed exact batch/UserOperation**, using thirdweb's installed signing/bundling primitives. Sign at the final checkout action, before starting payment. The payload must bind Arbitrum, donor wallet, token/input cap, conversion minimum if applicable, vault, donor share receiver, nonce and a bounded validity/cancellation policy. A relayer pays execution costs and submits only that instruction once provider delivery is independently confirmed. It receives no donor private key or unrestricted signing authority. Wallet creation/recovery and share ownership stay with the donor; Green Goods can sponsor fees without holding principal.

This is a feasibility candidate, not a verified feature. Prove whether an unfunded account can prepare the operation, how simulation and sponsorship work, how long the signature/paymaster quote remains usable, and what happens after 3DS or delayed delivery. Show that expiration cannot trigger a silently changed operation or a second charge. If this cannot work, timebox evaluation of a restricted thirdweb session permission; only accept one that enforces recipient, calldata/amount, expiry and single-use bounds onchain. The ordinary ERC-4337 [approved-target session setting](https://portal.thirdweb.com/transactions/session-keys) alone does not restrict an ERC-20 transfer's recipient/amount. New [granular session documentation](https://portal.thirdweb.com/wallets/session-keys) uses EIP-7702 examples; Arbitrum compatibility is not established by that example. No wildcard session and no app-held admin key.

If neither method works with existing deployed components, the one-checkout requirement is blocked. Browser-open automatic signing may be useful diagnostic evidence but does not pass reliable background completion. A pre-signed USDC transfer alone is insufficient: it makes a direct donation, not the selected endowment.

Each payment route needs an explicit settlement window and a compatible authorization lifetime. In particular, a bank route taking business days cannot inherit a card operation's short-lived sponsorship or swap quote. Requote only within the donor's enforced bounds. On expiry, stop and preserve donor funds; do not silently broaden access, re-sign, charge again or ask for a second deposit confirmation and count that as success. A safe recoverable failure can still be a no-go for this route.

## Funds and confirmation

Keep separate observed states: checkout ready → donor authorization recorded → card/payment pending → crypto delivered to donor → endowment submitted → endowment confirmed. Failure, pending and expired outcomes retain their meaning; map them to the existing funding-intent model during implementation rather than inventing a parallel production lifecycle here.

Server-side idempotency prevents duplicate purchase/deposit attempts; authorization nonce and chain receipts protect value movement. Verify provider session/customer/wallet/chain/asset/amount binding and actual delivery, then the vault's deposit receipt and positive donor share delta. Confirm the garden's configured yield entitlement separately: deposited principal is not immediately spendable garden cash. Inspect the funded strategy/beneficiary and demonstrate one small yield allocation/claim when possible; report pending yield proof honestly.

If payment succeeds but deposit fails or expires, keep funds in the donor's wallet, show “payment received; endowment pending,” preserve email recovery, and stop retries that exceed consent. No success screen from a webhook alone. No automatic card-refund promise after crypto delivery. Green Goods owns the support entry point and checkout/deposit recovery; the payment provider owns purchase/KYC issues under the agreed escalation arrangement. Send one Green Goods endowment confirmation alongside the provider's purchase receipt. Redeeming shares to the donor's wallet does not prove redemption to a local bank account; local off-ramping is outside this pilot and must not be promised.

Show the local payment total, provider fee, FX/conversion costs, minimum endowment amount and expected wait before payment. Keep a stablecoin quote distinct from a USD payment amount. Treat optional [Stripe fee subsidization](https://docs.stripe.com/crypto/onramp/fee-subsidization) as account-gated. Verify issuer cash-advance treatment after posting; a checkout quote cannot reveal every issuer charge. Do not claim that a displayed zero fee eliminates FX spread.
