# SustainableFinance.Live: pitch research and revision rationale

Researched 23 October 2026 Pacific. Scope: the official 2026 challenge and agenda, organizer
coverage of the 2023–2025 winners, winner-interview descriptions and the 2026 conference. This
research informs the existing Green Goods pitch. It does not replace its broader story with the
TAS cohort or change the accepted architecture.

## Recommendation

Preserve the original sequence: the community funding gap, Green Goods, the member and supporter
journeys, hub/community/diaspora/institutional support, Nigeria, the funding offer and growth.
Use the TAS three-person project to make those ideas concrete. Lead the funding ask with one
cohort and price coordination separately.

The central proposition is that communities can coordinate useful work and service capacity,
then give supporters a clearer account of delivery. Vouchers extend members' access to that
capacity. They belong inside this story, alongside the existing funding routes.

The recommendation below is an **inference from published evidence**, not a claim that we know
the 2026 judges' private preferences or can predict a winning entry.

## How this changes the pitch without replacing it

| Original part | Preserve | Weave in |
|---|---|---|
| Opening and hub | Community funding gap; TAS's solar hub, power, internet, learning and member priorities | Member services and collaboration as concrete capacity already present |
| Green Goods | Requests, offers, commitments, evidence, confirmation and existing funding routes | Vouchers as service access; G$ as optional money on its independent Celo route |
| Member journey | Join, do useful work, receive the agreed reward, access services, return | TAS designer/analyst/engineer example with three separate commitments |
| Supporter journey | Clear recipient, confirmed delivery, outstanding obligations and a readable record | Cohort and services funded, coordination cost, remedies and renewal decision |
| Voucher explanation | Issuer obligation and delivery distinct from holding or transfer | Member and garden ownership, one shared pool, one peer exchange, consent and escrow |
| Four levels of support | Hub, community, diaspora and institutions; resources in and evidence out | Local reciprocal exchange alongside external support, with member ownership preserved |
| Nigeria | Existing relationships and diaspora participation | A dated, sourced national context figure and the narrow Omo Yoruba starting route |
| Funding offer | Separate hub and coordination budgets | Sponsor one TAS cohort first; confirm actual price and capacity before collecting funds |
| Status and growth | Honest evidence and a path to repeat support and other hubs | Live account proof versus collective rehearsal versus pending member-flow proof |
| Closing | First supporter, next supporter, partners and feedback | A concrete cohort ask within the broader Green Goods opportunity |

The analyst's peer data review is an additional future service obligation. It must remain
separate from the funded analyst project deliverable. Likewise, the engineer's peer-help service
is distinct from the funded engineering deliverable. This avoids counting the same work twice.
The designer can participate in the project without issuing a personal voucher.

## The questions the pitch must answer

These are our preparation questions, derived from the evidence above. They are not official
2026 judging weights.

1. **Need:** What useful work or service is difficult to coordinate or fund today, and whose
   experience demonstrates it? Use the TAS member themes without inventing quotes.
2. **Product:** What changes for the member and supporter? Show the request, agreed reward,
   service access and delivery record in ordinary language.
3. **Financial mechanism:** Who promises the service, who funds cash costs, who owns the voucher,
   what does exchange do, and what happens when delivery is incomplete?
4. **Business model:** Which program budget holder pays for onboarding, coordination and
   reporting? What real result would make that payer renew? Pricing and demand remain to test.
5. **Technical feasibility:** Which parts are working, locally rehearsed or still being built?
   Use the selected live proof accurately, without making it stand for the whole service flow.
6. **Sustainability:** How does the system support useful local skills and regenerative work?
   Measure actual outcomes. A website project alone proves neither carbon reduction nor
   biodiversity improvement.
7. **Repeatability:** What must a second hub supply: local owners, service terms, capacity,
   onboarding and funding? What stays reusable in Green Goods?

The distinctive contribution is the connection between local commitments, service access and
evidence for support. We should not add AI underwriting, environmental risk scores, carbon
claims or guaranteed liquidity merely because other winners used related technologies.

**UNRESOLVED.** The public 2026 sources and the available GROW-43 comments do not establish the
individual duration, scoring weights, submission template, required sandbox use or precise
demo format. The earlier internal London-time assumption is not an official cutoff.

**Recommendation.** Keep the full 13-slide main story and four appendices. Rehearse the 9:10
spoken version, plus a 6:45 compressed version with one minute for a demonstration and 15 seconds
of buffer if the organizer confirms an eight-minute combined slot. Retain the wider topics in
both versions. Use the appendix for chain architecture, authority and detailed evidence.

Before submitting, confirm the format with the organizer through the existing event channel.
This research did not send a message, submit an entry or change Linear.
