Green Goods: From Good Intentions to Green Outcomes

We built commitment pooling: communities promise useful work, prove it, and turn the record into support. The people a promise served confirm it was kept, never the people who did the work. We are starting with Tech and Sun, which builds solar-powered hubs in Nigeria; its first hub, in Awka, has served more than 300 people.

What we built for SF.Live: Cosmo-Local Credit inside commitment pooling

Commitment pooling was already live: members offer or request specific work, take promises up and document them, and the person served confirms each one kept. For SF.Live we integrated Cosmo-Local Credit, the open voucher protocol from Grassroots Economics, so a kept promise can earn service vouchers. When a promise is confirmed on Arbitrum, a cross-network message pays its vouchers on Gnosis, where Cosmo-Local runs. Members swap them in one shared Cosmo-Local pool and book services, with vouchers held in escrow until delivery and undelivered units returned. The contracts are deployed on public networks and in pilot with Tech and Sun, and the whole flow was rehearsed end to end, including what happens when a step fails partway.

Code: github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods · the voucher adapter and escrow contracts

The problem

Communities create value every season, but the record of it stays trapped. Proving work costs more than doing it, reporting tools were built for offices, and the record sits on platforms communities don't own.

Where it stands

Live: the app, promises, chat reporting and endowments. Deployed: the voucher contracts, on public networks since 24 October. In pilot: service vouchers with Tech and Sun. Open now: the hub's first promise season, running to 31 January 2027.

Accessible by design

Works offline, signs in by fingerprint or face, reports by chat, in English, Spanish or Portuguese.

Reporting costs less

Proof is captured as the work happens, confirmed by the person served, and read by every funder.

How it pays for itself

Funders pay Green Goods a separate fee for setup, support and reporting. Endowments earn DeFi yield on ETH and stablecoins, typically 2 to 7% a year and not guaranteed. Green Goods' share is 2.7% by default, being switched on. Never a supporter's deposit, never a garden's shared fund.

Who we are looking for

Institutional funders who want to pilot allocation tools like Green Goods, diaspora associations ready to pool members' gifts behind a hub's season, and mentors for business development and for telling our story.
