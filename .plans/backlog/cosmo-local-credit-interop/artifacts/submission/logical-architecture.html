Logical architecture · Green Goods · October 2026 · Written for SustainableFinance.Live 2026, Problem Statement 2

One record, from the field to the networks that fund and count it.

Five roles, five surfaces, one set of Green Goods services on open protocols and three public networks. For Problem Statement 2, it makes a community's commitments and history visible: promises, work and confirmations become one record for fair finance, mutual credit and better coordination.

People

Five roles in every garden

GardenersDo the work and document it, offline when needed.
StewardsRun the garden: seasons, members, review and approval.
EvaluatorsSet the baseline and author assessments.
FundersFund gardens directly or through endowments.
Community membersWitness the work and confirm what was done for them.

Green Goods services

One record, one set of rules, on every surface

AccountsPasskey accounts, sponsored fees, one address on every network.
Gardens and rolesGarden accounts, roles as Hats, membership and joining.
PromisesOffers and requests, take-up, proof, confirmation by the person served.
Work and evidenceSubmissions and approvals as signed attestations; media on IPFS.
AssessmentsBaselines and outcomes an evaluator records for each area of work.
RewardsAgreed G$ rewards for kept promises; service purchases across gardens.
Service vouchersIssuer tokens, an adapter that pays earned vouchers, one pool, escrow.
EndowmentsETH and stablecoin DeFi vaults, about 2 to 7% a year, not guaranteed; earnings to garden, certificates and, once switched on, protocol.
Impact certificates and payoutsA season's impact as a certificate; campaign payouts.
Indexer and public dataReads Arbitrum and Celo today, Gnosis next, so pages, reports and partners share one record.

Data in

  • Evidence from the field: photos, notes, audio and links, by app or by chat, captured offline and synced later.
  • Confirmations by the people served, and steward review of work.
  • Baselines and assessments from evaluators.
  • Accessible by default: offline capture, passkeys, chat and three languages, so reporting costs less.

Data out

  • Public garden pages and the indexer's data, readable by anyone.
  • Reports funders can act on and renew from.
  • Records published to partner networks: Ma Earth through Certified first; Bloom and Regen in conversation.
  • Impact certificates for a season's work; the rewards and vouchers a kept promise earns.

Rules that hold everywhere

  • The person served confirms a promise, never the person who did it; a steward can confirm only as a labeled fallback.
  • Work, approvals and assessments are signed attestations anyone can check; other records are contract state.
  • Messages between networks authorize actions; they never carry money or tokens.
  • Payment commands between networks carry a stable identity, so a retry never repeats one. Contracts can pause; upgrades need owner approval.

Status on 25 October 2026: the app, promises, chat reporting and endowments are live; the voucher contracts are deployed on the public networks and in pilot with Tech and Sun; impact certificates are partly built (minting exists, buying in the app does not yet); voucher recovery and members' approval of delegated authority are still being built.
