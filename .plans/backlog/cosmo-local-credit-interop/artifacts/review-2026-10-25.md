## Structure changes

### New slide: the solution, before commitment pooling

Add a high-level solution slide between the problem (2) and commitment pooling (now 4). It says
what Green Goods solves and introduces the vocabulary the rest of the deck leans on: a **garden**
is a place-based community on Green Goods; **gardeners** do and document the work; **stewards**
run the garden; and the record the garden keeps is what supporters read. Keep it to the shape of
the product and the words, not features. Candidate headline: "Green Goods gives a community one
record of what it promised, did and proved."

### Five roles: before or after the loop

Open question Afo raised, tied to the solution slide. Recommendation: once the solution slide
introduces garden, gardener and steward, move the roles **before** the loop, so the loop can use
the role names without defining them: problem, solution, roles, loop, commitment pooling,
vouchers. If the slide count gets tight, fold the roles into the solution slide as a compact list
and keep the loop as the first diagram. Decide locally; both orders are coherent, the first is
easier to narrate.

### Slide 3 · Commitment pooling: a fourth bullet

Add a bullet on collaboration and exchange between members of a hub: today people cannot see
the skills, time, equipment and services others nearby could offer. Promises make those offers
and requests visible to each other and matchable. Candidate copy: "Members see what others can
offer and need, so skills and help move between them."

### Slide 4 · The loop: reciprocity in the Promise step

Rewrite the Promise step so it shows promises running between members, not only from a member
to the garden. Candidate copy: "Members promise work to each other and to the garden, and agree
who does what by when."

### Slide 5 · "No one confirms their own work"

Afo is unsure the line belongs on the roles slide. Why it was there: it is the one-sentence
integrity rule behind the credibility argument on slide 8 and behind Problem Statement 2's
"trustworthy and investable" question. The precise rule is that the person who did the work can
never confirm it, the person served confirms, and a steward fallback is possible but recorded as
a fallback. Recommendation: drop it from the roles slide and keep one trust line where it earns
its place, on the commitment pooling confirm bullet or in slide 8's closing line, phrased "the
person who did the work never confirms it."

### Slide 6 · Cosmo-Local vouchers

Remove the status line "Rehearsed end to end. The live deployment is under way." The goal is to
have the voucher flow live by the presentation, so the slide should not hedge. Say nothing about
deployment status on the slide; the notes' claims list and the brief's evidence box carry the
truth on the day. Also remove the same phrase from the notes for slides 6 and 14 and from brief
pages 1, 3 and 6.

### Slide 7 · Tech and Sun is an initiative, not one hub

The deck presents Tech and Sun as a single solar hub. It is an initiative to build solar-powered
hubs; the Awka hub is the first, live one. Reframe: introduce Tech and Sun as the initiative,
then focus on the Awka hub. Candidate headline: "We're starting with Tech and Sun, which builds
solar-powered hubs in Nigeria. The first is live in Awka." Research below.

### Slide 9 · Four levels of capital

- Headline: "Hubs are rich in capital, but short of money" (plural).
- Do not color "financial" green in the legend; treat it like the other forms.
- Make the Community, Nigerian diaspora and Institutions entries larger; there is white space to
  use on the right.
- Make the rings easier to match to the legend: give each ring a distinct fill and reuse that fill
  in the legend glyph, so the eye can connect "Community" to its ring.
- Community adds **material** capital (spaces, local businesses, equipment). The diaspora could
  also bring material capital; if the legend stays at four forms per level, keep the four that
  matter most for each.

### Slide 11 · The Nigerian diaspora

Stop describing Omo Yoruba of Southern California as Tech and Sun's fiscal sponsor, now that Tech
and Sun is the NGO. Keep it as the first diaspora community. Same change in the notes, in brief

### Slide 13 · Business model

Say **garden** where the slide says "community", since the deck has grounded its terminology by

### Slide 14 · Where we are and where next

## Tech and Sun research · 25 October 2026

Read through web search only; techandsun.com itself was unreachable from the cloud session, so
verify every line against the site before use. Sources: the
[Tech & Sun site](https://techandsun.com/), the
[Tech & Sun round by Greenpill Nigeria](https://hub.regencoordination.xyz/t/tech-sun-round-by-greenpill-nigeria/393)
on the Regen Coordination hub, and the
[hub activation listing](https://luma.com/au47b6cr) at Unizik.

- **What it is.** The site describes Tech and Sun (TAS) as building "dependable solar-powered
  hubs that give students, builders, and local communities in Nigeria steady access to
  electricity and internet", a "space to learn, build, and grow together".
- **Where.** The first hub is at Nnamdi Azikiwe University (Unizik) in Awka and is described as
  live. The Greenpill Nigeria round page also names the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, as a
  second university; the site's roadmap does not, so treat Nsukka as unconfirmed.
- **Roadmap.** The site describes "a grounded path from the live Awka hub toward a broader
  network of university-based hubs across Nigeria", with 2026 milestones of launching at Unizik,
  bringing the model to Enugu, and breaking ground in Lagos and Abuja. The ledger's C01 says not
  to present those cities as a record; present them as the initiative's plan, in its words.
- **What a hub offers.** Round-the-clock electricity from solar, high-speed Starlink internet, a
  solar-powered container facility, workspace, and digital education. The round page says the
  hubs host Ethereum staking node infrastructure; leave that off the pitch unless Afo wants it.
- **Partners.** Greenpill Nigeria is listed as a strategic partner and presents the activation
  events. The round is a community grant program.
- **Legal status.** Afo confirms Tech and Sun is now the NGO. The ledger's C05 still carries the
  registered-name placeholder; insert the registered string if the brief names it.

Suggested slide 7 copy: "Tech and Sun builds solar-powered hubs that give students, builders and
local communities in Nigeria steady power and internet. The first hub is live in Awka, at
Nnamdi Azikiwe University; Enugu, Lagos and Abuja are planned."
