## Purpose & goals

Pass 1 is a self-serve form each cohort garden completes before its onboarding call, so the call can be about action (confirming the mandate, planning platform onboarding, setting the first-Season timeline) instead of discovery. It walks a garden from the needs it is facing, through the commitments that could address them, to how kept commitments will be measured. It should leave the person filling it out with more clarity about their own garden's pattern than they started with.

Each section produces evidence for one of six research decisions:

1. **Seeding go/no-go per garden** — a mandate artifact each garden recognizes as its own plan.
2. **Ship-or-revise the vocabulary** — whether needs-as-things-to-solve, the need→outcome pairing, horizon, and stage-of-clarity language carry each garden's reality, and separately whether commitment direction (help asked for vs help given) lands where it belongs in Section 3. Misfit answers are the architecture-gap instrument.
3. **Which commitment units seed Cycle 1** — action-registry fit, missing work types, capacity and support bounds.
4. **Whether mutual credit earns a design lane** — existing member-to-member exchange plus lightly captured credit terms.
5. **What anchors measurement** — what each garden tracks, wants to track, and must report to funders.
6. **G$ rollout order** — per-garden reward relevance and how value should circulate.

Coordination-channel and join-flow observations feed RESR-64 (join-request persistence); that decision is not an outcome of this survey.

## How to field it

**Cohort.** Three confirmed candidate gardens: Tech and Sun Hub (Awka, Nigeria — English), Greenpill Cape Town (Muizenberg / Deep South Circles — English; UNICEF-funded trash-bin/waste-cleanup program; showcase folded in from canceled RESR-53), and AgroforestDAO / Redemption Hill (Bias Fortes, Brazil — Portuguese). Between them they cover all four action domains. A **fourth slot stays open** for a mature MRV-adoption anchor: candidates are under consideration, none is selected, and no artifact names one (commitment-pooling Decision Log #29, 2026-07-18). Naming a candidate does not presume readiness. Question 1.2 is free text, so the form carries no cohort list and any garden can answer it.

**A garden enters fielding only when all are named:**

- steward contact and backup contact;
- preferred language and accessible format (the onboarding call itself runs in English);
- community consent path for notes and any call recording;
- likely gathering date or remote session;
- one plausible need and one commitment candidate;
- whether G$ rewards are relevant for this garden now;
- interviewer, note-taker, and follow-up owner.

**Language.** English master. One Google Form per language: Portuguese is required for Bias Fortes. If a fourth garden is ever selected, its language is confirmed before any translation is prepared. Translation is a **fielding gate**. The onboarding call runs in English; stewards are welcome to bring a community translator.

**Vocabulary note.** This instrument says **steward** for the person who runs a garden, glossed "(operator in the app today)" on first use — matching the in-progress rename in the external brief.

### Build notes (Google Forms)

- One Form per language (English first; see Language above). The `## Section N` headings below map 1:1 to Forms sections — **`## Form header`, `## Branching map`, `## Privacy & consent` and `## Synthesis` are not Forms sections**, they are build guidance. Progress bar on.
- **Pasting options**: each Options / Rows / Columns block is fenced so it can be copied as a unit. Pasting multi-line text into the first option field splits it into separate options; if your build does not split them, paste one line per option.
- **Required / Other**: the type line under each heading states `Required` or `Optional` and whether the "Other" option is on.
- **Ranking questions**: Forms has no native ranking type. Questions marked `Multiple-choice grid (ranking)` use rows = the things being ranked, columns = `1st choice` / `2nd choice` / `3rd choice`, with "limit to one response per column" **on** so a respondent cannot rank two things first. Rows are not required, so they can rank only what matters to them.
- **Branching**: 4.8 uses "Go to section based on answer" — Yes → Section 4A, Not yet → Section 5. Section 4A ends by continuing to Section 5.
- **Do not turn on email collection.** 1.1/1.5 capture contact deliberately; the response sheet is research-notes storage with restricted access, never pasted into Linear.
- **Media**: upload each section's image as the Forms section-header image or an image item beside the named question. A YouTube link embeds natively as a Forms video item. Loom cannot embed, so the walkthrough link lives in the form description text.
- **Placeholders to insert before fielding**: the Grassroots Economics explainer URL (Section 3), the public commitment-pooling brief link (form header), the Google Calendar appointment link (5.2), and **the named support contact** — Sections 5 and the privacy notes both tell people to "tell your support contact" to retract answers, and that person is currently unnamed anywhere in the form.
- **Totals**: 49 items on the main path, +6 if the funder branch triggers. Four of the 49 are domain grids most gardens partly skip. Estimated 20–26 minutes.
- **Section 2 is about needs only — never about who will help.** Every question there is about the whole list (2.1, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 2.6, 2.7), the two or three that matter most (2.2), or the garden's own direction over the next few years (2.8). Nothing narrows to a single need, and nothing asks who would provide or receive help: that is Section 3's job. There are no per-need question blocks and no pre-set need categories, so the capture stays open and the synthesis prompt does the structuring.
- **Section 2 is also the theory-of-change section**, though it never uses the phrase. Cause (2.5), beneficiary (2.6), outcome and horizon (2.2), how the needs relate (2.3) and aspiration (2.8) are the tiers; the intervention is 3.6 and the indicators are 4.1. None of it is asked as theory — the synthesis prompt assembles it into a statement the facilitator reads back at the call.
- **Cut ladder** if piloting runs long: 3.14 first, then 3.13, then 1.9, then 2.7. **Never cut**: 2.1, 2.2, 2.5, 2.6 (they are theory-of-change tiers the read-back depends on), 3.6, 3.7, 4.1, 4.4 (named PRD-719 evidence), and the consent questions 5.4 / 5.5. 5.6 is a redaction question rather than a consent flag, but keep it — it is the only place someone can protect a third party.

## Privacy & consent

- **Three tiers, explained to participants**: nothing from this survey goes on-chain or public; responses live in restricted research-notes storage (the Form's response sheet plus call notes); anything retracted is deleted.
- Consent is **separate per use**: written notes (5.4) and quotations (5.5), recorded as enumerated values only.
- **We do not ask for photos, audio, or video** anywhere in this survey or the call. If someone sends media anyway, it stays in research notes and is never shared, published, or posted.
- The call can run **without any recording**; say so and we take written notes only.
- Participants may **retract research notes any time before synthesis** by telling the support contact.
- Hard boundary: grievances naming individuals, wallet addresses, join-request identities, and contact details never enter Linear, PostHog, or public artifacts. Analytics stores enumerated/boolean research metadata only. Raw response rows are never pasted into Linear.

## Synthesis

The runnable prompt that turns one submission into these artifacts is **Appendix B of `onboarding-call.md`**. It produces:

- **Need records** — one per need named in 2.1, enriched from 2.2 for the two or three that matter most (statement + desired outcome + horizon). A need carries **no kind**: it is something to solve, and direction lives on the commitment that answers it (3.7). These seed the needs board; the call fills any missing outcome or horizon in seconds.
- **One draft mandate row** from 3.6 (which need it addresses, who/what/for whom/by when) + 3.7 (direction: Request or Offer, which sets who confirms) + 2.2 (that need's outcome and horizon) + 3.1–3.4 (unit candidates) + 3.8 (capacity ceiling) + 4.1/4.7 (evidence, baseline author) + 1.6 (confirmation-group scale). Further rows are built **at the call** from the needs list, not invented from the form.
- **The garden's theory of change, in its own words** — assembled, never asked: because *[cause, 2.5]*, *[who, 2.6]* face *[the needs, 2.1]*; if *[the commitment, 3.6]* happens then *[outcome, 2.2]*, and we would know because *[indicator, 4.1]*; over *[horizon, 2.2]* this builds toward *[aspiration, 2.8]*, with *[how the needs relate, 2.3]* setting whether that happens in order or in parallel. The facilitator reads it back at the top of the call — a garden hearing its own logic stated cleanly is the clarity moment this instrument exists to create, and "no, that is not why" is the most useful correction we can get.
- **Onboarding roster** from 1.4/1.6/1.7/1.8 + 3.9 + 4.4: who becomes steward, gardener, or evaluator in the app; who takes the WhatsApp/SMS path; what training is needed.
- **Per-garden readiness row**: Participation (confirmed / invited / not ready / unavailable, never inferred — "not ready" is a respected outcome and belongs here whenever the garden says it) · Need examples · Commitment rows (confirmed vs draft) · Domains · Evidence and confirmation · G$ rewards · Circulation path · Open decisions with owner.
- **Open questions for the call** — the five most useful things to ask, ordered so the ones that unblock a commitment come first.
- **Gap log** — the architecture-gap instrument: any item in 2.1 that resists being framed as something to solve, any need whose horizon in 2.2 does not fit week / month / Season / years, "Not sure yet" on 3.7 (commitment direction), off-registry work (3.5), and 5.8 answers — routed to architecture review before August seeding. Prose that refuses our categories is stronger vocabulary evidence than a radio button ever was.

**Standing architecture gap** — tracked as **PRD-719, "Assessment deltas from accumulated work metrics"**: work submissions capture typed metrics per action, but nothing aggregates them (no Work entity in the indexer, `details` is an untyped record, hypercert bundling filters records rather than totalling them), so assessment deltas are computed by hand. **Section 4 is a named input to that issue** — what gardens track (4.1), what they wish they could track (4.3), where the information lives (4.4), and their funder-confidence rating (4.5) are the evidence its metric registry gets designed against. Route Section 4 answers there as well as into the readiness row.

**No cross-garden leaderboard, readiness score, or completeness percentage.** Scales are self-reports read within a garden only.
