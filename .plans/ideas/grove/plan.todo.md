# Grove: implementation sequence

**Status:** idea-stage product. Nothing below is built except the disposable G0 spike, and no step is complete. **Created:** 2026-10-11. **Updated:** 2026-11-14.
**MVP contract and decision log:** [spec.md](spec.md).
**Acceptance gates:** [eval.md](eval.md).

Each unchecked step is a future, independently verifiable checkpoint. Checkpoint IDs are stable: a step that moved keeps its ID, and the 18 earlier S/P/K IDs are preserved below. Proposed paths are not existing APIs. Split checkpoints into bounded changes after inspection; do not duplicate storage or capture work.

**Framing, corrected 14 November (D31 to D33).** This sequence makes Green Goods work locally on a phone, in a browser and with an extension. The C checkpoints build the local environment that the app and the extension share. The S, P and K checkpoints build Grove, the extension. A local garden is the unit throughout; where a step says "community" it means a garden's people. The F checkpoints, added the same day, put Green Goods' own functions on that environment, in the gardener app and the steward cockpit (D37 to D44).

## MVP order

The order follows three rules. The first is the owner's: "Local identity and key custody come first." The second is that the cheapest experiment able to change the design runs before anything is built on it. The third is the owner's order for the functions: the Green Goods chain, then promises, then joining and roles, then money and certificates (D39).

The MVP is the first version, and in it every current function works locally, in the gardener app and in the steward cockpit (D37). Each block is usable once its own gate passes.

Grove's lane can be built in parallel on synthetic records. It counts toward the [MVP finish line](spec.md#mvp-contract) only after steps 1 to 9 pass, and no private record enters it before then (D25).

**Next lanes, in the order they are likely to be needed:** direct sync between devices that are open at the same time (C10, D42); a hosted mailbox with Solid as the preferred adapter (C1a, C3); the Admin Hub snapshot and digest (S7, S9); KOI exchange (K1, K2); co-editing (C5); outside accounts and publication (C6); the wider scope of Milestone B (S10 to S12).

**Research, outside the MVP's gates:** EDV, Automerge + Keyhive (E25), MLS, Bluetooth sensors.

## Community-sharing delivery order

[The contract](community-sharing.md) owns the design; [the research](sharing-research.md) records what is unproven. This plan authorizes no implementation, dependency installation, tracker change or deployment. The entries are listed in the MVP order above, then the later lanes.

- [ ] **C0b — Key and capture durability on real devices.** New on 14 November (R5). On one physical phone and one physical laptop, with no protocol code, served from the alpha address (D46): create a key pair and a few captures in the Green Goods app installed to the Home Screen, in the same app in a browser tab, in the cockpit at its alpha address in a desktop browser, and in the packaged extension with a pinned ID (R12). Restart offline. Request persistent storage and record whether it is granted. Install it twice, or on two phones. Leave one unopened for the full fourteen days. Read the other once after eight days: that is past Safari's seven-day limit and early enough to change the design, and it leaves the fourteen-day run uninterrupted. Clear site data. Reinstall the extension. The alpha address shares a site with Green Goods' public pages, and Safari may count a visit to any of them as a visit to the app. So the idle phone opens no other `greengoods.app` page during the run, and one extra case records whether such a visit changes the result. Exit: E26. The result decides which phones may hold keys (R13). If the installed app cannot keep keys on a phone, the phone becomes a capture-only device that encrypts to the member's laptop key, and the plan changes before C0.
- [ ] **C0a — Passkey unlock, offline unlock and recovery on two devices.** A gate since 14 November (D19). Run [Pass A](collaboration-passkey-spike.md#pass-a-existing-passkey-offline-unlock-and-recovery) on real existing credentials from both populations: the `greengoods.app` credentials and the older per-site ones. The `greengoods.app` credentials are tested at the alpha addresses, which share that passkey domain (D46). The older per-site ones answer only on the site that created them or through the extension. Preserve the credential, its relying-party ID and the account recipe. Test the PRF secret on each passkey provider in use, the extension's route through host permission, a blocked network, a missing or refused prompt, a lost device, a lost credential, a corrupt export and a removed device whose passkey has synced back. Exit: E24. A provider that returns no secret gets the passphrase path; that passes the design and fails passkey unlock for that provider. No account migration, no new credential and no transaction.
- [ ] **C1 — Encrypted vault and shared files.** Choose between age v1 and HPKE inside a named, reviewed multi-recipient envelope, on what the supported devices can run (R1, O3), and record the library, version, license and any audit. Define the signed manifest, the wraps of the vault key, how device private keys are wrapped under it when they are created and imported after unlock, and capture while locked (R15). Name the construction that combines the passkey's secret with the device-held key, with its domain separation, store its version and parameters beside each wrap, and include derivation and upgrade vectors that the app and the extension both pass. State one at-rest envelope for a capture, the same whether the vault was locked or unlocked when it was made, and say when, if ever, a locked capture is rewrapped. Name the passphrase derivation and its cost, give each wrap its own random salt stored beside it, and measure it on the supported phone and laptop. Produce test vectors. Prove that a device that was not named cannot open a file; that a tampered or re-addressed file is rejected, and that a replayed one changes nothing and gets the receipt it got the first time; that a copied browser profile without the unlock secret cannot open the vault; that no stored key can sign or decrypt while the vault is locked; that no file name or content identifier of a private record reaches telemetry (R7); and that an exported file carries a random name, with nothing outside the encrypted part that names its content, its source or a person. Exit: E16. No private pilot before this gate.
- [ ] **C0 — Identity, pairing, the member list and recovery.** Define the member list as one signed, hash-chained history with one active signer (R3, D18) and no identifier scheme beyond public keys (R2). Fix the canonical bytes that every hash and signature in it covers, with test vectors that the app and the extension both pass. Prove enrollment with no provider or wallet; pairing by scanned code and a spoken comparison string, each enrollment bound to a fresh one-time challenge that is used up, so a removed device cannot replay the pairing that enrolled it; a device added to an existing member, where the list version carries a request signed by one of that member's own devices, or after a recovery a proof only that member holds, such as a signature by a recovery key that the export holds and the list names, and every device checks it, so the controller's signature alone attaches nothing; a removal, which retires that key with the member's place, so an old export adds nobody afterwards and a removed person who returns is admitted anew; a re-admission by the steward alone, for a member who has lost every device and their recovery code, as its own kind of version that removes the member's earlier devices and that every member sees, whose device can approve sharing only for records made after it was added, which a device checks without a clock because the record itself must be signed by a device enrolled at or after that re-admission (D53); a forged member or device entry; removal; the seven-day limit, a limit a community has changed, how an unchanged list stays fresh, and how a community whose devices are all stale shares again (D24); a device clock set back or far ahead, where a device keeps the latest time it has seen across restarts and pauses sharing while its clock reads earlier than that; a second controller, its takeover when the first controller's devices and code are lost, and refusal of a fork with a stated way back to one head (O6); the recovery code appointing a new controller device, which retires that code's succession key and issues a new one; a device that has never seen an appointment, which cannot tell a retired key from a live one and takes the current head only by pairing; when a recovery export is made and refreshed, and what a restore shows as missing when records were added after the last one; and separate device keys per community (W3). Exit: E15 and the local cases of E23. Synthetic fixtures only.
- [ ] **C2 — Capture → review → share offline, in the app and in Grove.** Adapt the existing revision and command design for local workspace identifiers and encrypted payloads. Build work reports and promises, through the existing screens and free-form, by voice or in writing (D34, D49); note, voice note, photo, file and link capture in the app and in Grove, and page capture in Grove; manual personal review; the author's share approval, signed by the author's device, bound to a digest of the exact content and checked for withdrawal at every send (D17, R6, O7), with the stated limit that a withdrawal made on one of the author's devices stops the others only once it reaches them; file export and import, with a prepared file's recipients checked against the member list the sender now holds at every send, including a retry, so one wrapped to a device that list no longer names is rebuilt without it or not sent, which adds no recipient and needs no new approval; the recipient's checks and signed receipt; review decisions and corrections; the author's fresh approval before a corrected version leaves; and a steward-approved partner summary as a new projection, carrying the steward's signed approval. Move captures from phone to laptop by file (D21). Use one work report, one promise and one knowledge source, and take each capture type through the loop once. No model, wallet, account or network. Exit: E17, E2 and the applicable parts of E1, E3 and E14, with no false Work, WorkApproval or publication state. Coordinate with S2 to S4, S6 and S8 instead of creating another store.
- [ ] **C4 — Phone boundary and device list.** Reduced on 14 November: direct transfer between devices is its own next lane (C10) and no longer part of this step. Record the exact browsers and devices that pass, what phone suspension does to an export or import in progress, file size and retry limits, how the share sheet and file import behave on iOS and Android, and power use. Exit: E19 for the file carrier. No mesh or background-availability claim. Sensors remain import fixtures until separately scoped.
- [ ] **C1a — Hosted mailbox: the store interface, then Solid.** A later lane, after direct sync (D21, D42). Define the store as opaque objects with put, get, list and delete by an unguessable name, and prove it first with any plain folder. Then follow [the two-device storage contract](storage-comparison-spike.md) on Solid with client-side encryption and local keys. Prove recovery, scoped access, host privacy with the operator's full view, migration and interruptions; record reused and new code, services, licenses and bundle cost. Exit: E22 and the applicable parts of E15 to E19. EDV stays an optional research variant. A failing privacy, permission or recovery gate blocks private use of that adapter; file exchange remains.
- [ ] **C3 — Solid adapter in the clients.** Next lane, after C1a. Pin provider, client and auth versions; prove sign-in and redirect in the extension and the Green Goods app, CORS and CSP, token recovery and suspension. Store ciphertext, key wraps and manifests only; reconcile revisions and receipts; change provider without changing local identity; test the operator's full access with storage permissions bypassed. Exit: E18 and E22. A Pod sign-in never becomes identity or key authority.
- [ ] **C5 — Co-editing with Yjs; Automerge + Keyhive research.** Later lane, opened when two people must edit one draft at once (D16). Use the Yjs core for merging, one new document per audience, with every key holder able to write (R9). Prove encrypted updates, offline edits, restart and reconnect, removed and stale members, history and presence privacy, and recovery on a physical phone and laptop. Exit: E20; a server that can read the document fails it. [Pass B](collaboration-passkey-spike.md#pass-b-yjs-versus-automerge--keyhive) and E25 are backlog research and gate nothing (R11). MLS is a separate, conditional evaluation.
- [ ] **C6 — Outside accounts and publication.** Later lane. Prove private, separately approved associations with a garden or a service account. Verify EOAs and ERC-1271 accounts and the current garden role, and prove a garden link through a Hat-wearing account (R8). The minimum proof for bringing an existing garden local moved to C8 on 14 November; C6 keeps the wider account associations. Record a release approval before anything is handed to an upload queue, bound to a digest of the exact payload (R6), together with the author's own approval of that payload when it carries their capture (D17). Both approvals are checked again at every upload, including a retry, and a withdrawn one sends nothing. A record published later carries its own times, and the chain's time shows only when it was published. Today's Work and WorkApproval resolvers judge an Action's window at the moment of attestation, so publishing work done inside a window after it has ended needs them to accept a stated time: a contract change for this lane, with its own authorization (D54, O20). D45's exception covers a steward's report to a named funder or partner, not a public upload. Test Certified's EOA-only limit instead of claiming GardenAccount support. Scope ATProto/Certified, Ethereum and archival adapters one at a time with their owning projects. Exit: E21 and the outside-account cases of E23; no chain transaction or public upload is authorized by this plan.

**First complete demonstration:** a steward starts a garden with no wallet, defines two Actions and writes the assessment in the cockpit's local build → a gardener reports work on a phone, once through the work screen and once in their own words → pairs their laptop and carries the reports across as a file → on the laptop, Grove's agent proposes the structured version of the free-form report beside the app's screen, and the gardener accepts it → the gardener shares both reports with the steward → the steward approves one and rejects the other with feedback → the decisions return to the gardener's phone → the steward sends a separately approved report to a named funder. Then a promise is made, taken up and confirmed; a second gardener joins; and a cookie-jar claim is approved offline and settles only once a device connects. Alongside, the agent brings in a funding call that fits the garden's assessment while the gardener browses, and shows why it was brought in, where it came from and what to check before sharing. Nothing is uploaded, hosted or signed in to until that claim settles.

## Function blocks

Added 14 November (D37 to D44). These checkpoints put Green Goods' own functions on the loop the C checkpoints prove. Each one runs in the local builds of the gardener app and the steward cockpit, keeps today's rules, and uses today's screens. None of them needs a connection, except the settlement step of F7, and none publishes anything. Where they say a steward, an owner can do the same, as today. Each block starts with an inventory of every action its screens and contracts offer today, taken from the code. The block's gate covers that whole list, leaving an action out of the first version takes the owner's recorded decision, without which the gate does not pass, and the cases written below are examples (D48).

- [ ] **C7 — Separate local builds of the app and the cockpit.** (D37, D44) Publish the gardener app and the steward cockpit as local builds at their own addresses under `greengoods.app`: the same screens, no analytics and no calls to Green Goods' hosted services. A build loads and updates its own files from its own address. Beyond that, the only network use is the connected read and the one onchain record of an imported garden (C8, D47), and a settlement (F7), each started by a person and each through endpoints that E30 lists. The existing passkey reaches them through the shared domain (W2). Prove by inspecting the network that a local build makes no request carrying a private record, or the file name or content identifier of one, that it makes none at all once installed and offline, and that the existing public upload queue cannot be reached from it. They are developed and published at `alpha.greengoods.app` and `alpha.admin.greengoods.app`, not in the beta environment (D46). Today a deployed build knows two brandings, staging and production, and always uses the Agent's passkey directory. So the alpha builds need a third name and icon, and they work without the directory. An existing passkey still answers there, because a deployed build names `greengoods.app` as its passkey domain and the alpha addresses are under it. Settle where the module shared with Grove lives (O2). Exit: E30. O8, what limits a code update, still gates sensitive records.
- [ ] **C8 — Start a garden locally, or bring an existing one local.** (D33, D38, D47) A steward starts a garden with no chain: its name, place and first roles become the first signed member list (C0). For a garden that already exists onchain, one connected read brings its public roles, Actions and current assessment onto a steward's device. The read uses public sources only: the chain, the public attestation index through which the app finds assessments, and IPFS gateways for the files an assessment or an Action names. Every fetched file, including an assessment's nested metrics file, is checked against the content identifier that names it before anything is signed, and one that does not match is refused. The index only finds a record and never vouches for it: before the importer signs, each imported assessment is read from the chain itself, where it must exist under one of Green Goods' assessment schemas, name this garden as its recipient and not be revoked. The resolver behind those schemas refused any author who did not hold the role that kind of assessment needs, so an attestation that exists under them had an authorized author when it was made. Each imported Action is confirmed against the Action registry on the chain: the entry exists with those fields, and its domain is one the garden has enabled. State here the rule that picks the current assessment, taken from the code. Today's app reads a garden's roles and Actions through Green Goods' own indexer, which a local build does not call (D44), so C8 reads them from the chain or brings that one exception to the owner. That steward first proves control of an account that wears the garden's steward or owner Hat. They sign a domain-separated challenge with a nonce, an audience and an expiry. The challenge names the garden, the chain and the public key of the device that will sign the first member list, so the account's signature ties that key to that garden. An EOA or an ERC-1271 account is verified by its own path, and the role is checked fresh for that exact garden and chain (R8). The result is recorded as a protocol authority observation, naming the account, the garden and chain, the Hat checked, the block and the time, and the observation and the signed challenge are part of the first local member list, so the list's identifier covers them. The Actions and the assessment come in as imported records, not as part of the list. An imported record keeps its onchain source, meaning the attestation or registry entry, its author's account and the block, and it is signed by the importing steward's device as importer, never as author. The public roles come in as an observation too: which accounts hold which role at that block. They make nobody a local member, because a member is a set of enrolled device keys. The first list holds the importing steward. Every other person joins by pairing (F6), and the steward's device gives them their role with the observation shown beside the choice. Tying a member to their onchain account is the later association lane (C6). A first list signed by any other key is refused. The import then records the local garden's identifier onchain, written by the importing account through the same transport as a settlement, and it is complete only once that record is confirmed (D47). A later connected read by another steward finds the record, and that steward joins the garden by pairing instead of creating a second one. Where two records are written in a race, the earlier one stands. An owner of the garden can replace the record, so a first importer who pairs nobody cannot lock the garden out (D51), and the garden is then carried over (D52). The owner gets the garden's member list by pairing with any member's device, and takes over from the newest head they can get. They prove control of an account that wears the garden's owner Hat with the same kind of challenge as an import, sign a takeover version from that head that makes their own device the signer, and write a replacement record that names the garden's identifier and that version. The takeover is complete once that record is confirmed. Members, roles and records stay as they are. The previous signer stops being the signer and stays a member until a later version removes it. The record names the takeover version, so a device that has checked the record follows that version: anything the previous signer signed that the takeover does not descend from, whether a rival version from the same head or later ones the owner never received, is set aside and shown, never merged, and the owner's device signs again whatever should stand. A device follows an owner's takeover only after it has itself checked the onchain record and the Hat. One that cannot connect holds the takeover and shows it, and its member takes it by pairing again with a device they trust, so a takeover that arrives by file with no record the device can check changes nothing. A later replacement continues from the head the earlier one left, and one that does not is refused and shown. Where no member's device can give the owner the list, there is nothing to carry: the replacement names a new garden, and a device of the old one keeps every record it holds, shows that the onchain record now names another garden, and joins that garden only by pairing. No replacement switches a device silently. The record shows which account brought the garden local and when, and after a takeover which owner's account took it over and when, and the importing steward or the owner sees that before confirming. The record is an attestation under one new schema with no resolver (D55), which C11 registers, and C8 does not pass before it. Design the record's fields here, including how it names a takeover version without naming a member. Other members take the garden the way every member joins one: by pairing with a device they trust, which gives them the list's identifier (C0), and a connected device checks that identifier against the onchain record. A device that already holds the identifier refuses a different first list for the same garden and shows it, never merging. The garden works offline from then on. A member who never connects cannot check the Hat or a contract account's signature, so offline their device shows the observation as not yet checked and relies on the pairing. Any device that does connect checks the signature and the role again. This minimum proof moves here from C6. Exit: E31. The read is public data only and brings no work history; the Hub snapshot stays S7.
- [ ] **C11 — The schema for a local garden's record.** (D55) The record of a local garden's identifier, and an owner's replacement of it, are attestations under one new schema with no resolver and no new contract code, registered once on each chain where a garden is brought local. Fix its fields with C8, and add it to the contracts package's schema configuration and registration through that package's own wrappers and checks. Nothing onchain limits who writes under it, so the rule lives on the device: a record counts only when its writer wore the garden's steward or owner Hat at the block it was written, a replacement only when its writer wore the owner Hat, and anything else is ignored. A record whose writer has since lost the Hat still counts. Checking a past block needs a node that keeps old state. Where a device cannot make that check, it shows the record as not yet checked and starts no second local garden over it without the steward's explicit choice. Registering the schema is an onchain transaction and a change to a critical surface: it needs the owner's go-ahead for each chain, and a test network comes first. A resolver or contract that refuses the write onchain is the stronger later step. Exit: the schema is registered on a test network and E31 runs against it. No registration on a production chain is authorized by this plan.
- [ ] **C9 — Bridge between the local builds and Grove.** (D41) A content script limited to the exact addresses of the local builds relays a typed bridge between the page and Grove. The app or the cockpit passes the record under review. Grove returns the structured version, the agent's assessment and insights, and they are shown beside the app's own screen. Check the sender, the origin, the top frame and the message shape at every boundary. Every request and response names the garden and the record revision it is about. Each side refuses a message for a garden other than the one it has open, matches each response to its request, and drops one that arrives after the person has switched garden or record. Grove never receives keys, and the page never receives Grove's knowledge captures unless the person shares them. C9 owns this relay. S7 was designed around the same one and extends it to the Hub's address when that lane opens. Exit: E32. Which side holds the garden's keys and records on a laptop is designed here (O17).
- [ ] **F1 — Actions and the assessment.** (D39, D43) In the cockpit's local build, a steward defines the Actions gardeners can report, and a steward or evaluator writes the assessment: baseline, method, outcome targets, selected Actions and reporting period, as today. Both are garden records, signed by their author's device and shared by file. Records imported from an existing garden keep their onchain source instead (C8). Each assessment names the one or ones it replaces, and a steward settles two that replace the same one with an assessment that names both. A steward can edit an Action's title, reporting window and instructions as one new signed revision, and a report made against an older revision is kept and flagged at review. The current assessment is the agent's focus, with any extra topics a steward adds. Exit: E33.
- [ ] **F2 — Work.** (D34) The existing work screen runs in the local build against the garden's local Action list, beside the free-form path that C2 proves. That screen keeps its steps. Media stay as they are, and in place of the details form a member can record a voice note or write freely (D49). Through the form, a report needs the Action's required fields and minimum photos, as today. A free-form report is saved as it is, and only its structured version can be approved: what the Action requires and the note does not say is shown as missing until the gardener or the reviewer adds it, and an approval attempted before then is refused. An Action's window is judged by when the work happened, as the report says, so a note recorded after the window about work done inside it counts (D54). Each report carries how that time was established: the person's word, a photo's capture time and when the device saved the report. The reviewer sees all three, and any mismatch between them. A gardener follows each report on their own device: saved, shared, in review, approved or rejected, with the steward's feedback. Exit: E17 and the status cases of E34.
- [ ] **F3 — Approval.** A steward approves or rejects a work report in the app or the cockpit, with feedback, confidence and verification method, as today. The decision is signed by the steward's device and carries the time it was signed, nobody approves their own work, and the decision returns to the gardener by file. A steward can approve after an Action's window for work done inside it (D54). Two stewards who decide differently on the same revision leave the work disputed on every device, in whatever order the decisions arrive, as disagreeing decisions do today. It stays out of a report until a later decision by a steward or an owner names both. Exit: E34.
- [ ] **F4 — The report for a funder.** At the end of a period a steward builds a report from approved work reports and the assessment's targets, placing work in the period by when it happened and showing how that time was established (D54), approves it as a release (D17) and sends it to a named funder or partner as an encrypted file. The steward's approval is enough to include an approved work report in a revision its author shared or approved (D45). A version that someone else corrected or structured goes in only once its author has approved it (D17, D35). The funder reads it without receiving the evidence under it. Exit: E35.
- [ ] **F5 — Promises.** Offers and asks, take-up, proof or linked work, and confirmation, as signed decisions among the garden's devices, through the existing screens or free-form (D34). A steward sets up and seeds the pool and its seasons and campaigns in the cockpit, and runs them through today's states: a pool is opened, paused with a reason, resumed, closed and composted, and a season or a campaign is seeded, opened, closed and composted, or cancelled with a reason. Campaigns may overlap, and one season is open at a time. Today's rules hold: a provider or contributor cannot confirm their own promise, a confirmation cannot be taken back, a promise is fulfilled only when its confirmations reach its threshold, a steward's fallback confirmation needs a reason and applies only when ordinary confirmation cannot be reached, and the commitment limit applies. A due date is judged by when the proof happened, not when it reached another device (D54). State here how take-ups are ordered without the chain, by one device that orders them or by a rule that gives every device the same answer, so that merged records never leave a provider with more open commitments than the limit. A promise also ends as today: its creator withdraws it before take-up, a steward cancels it with a reason, it expires once it is due, and a dispute is raised with a reason by its creator, its counterparty, a named confirmer or a steward and resolved by a steward with a reason. Exit: E36.
- [ ] **F6 — Joining and roles.** A person joins an open garden with no steward's decision, or asks and waits for a steward to accept. A steward grants and revokes roles. A member who is not an owner can leave, which gives up all their roles in one step by their own signed decision, and an owner cannot leave, as today. A steward or owner edits the garden's settings, its name, description, location, banner, open joining, gardener limit and domains, and each edit is one signed revision of the garden's record. Each change to membership is a new version of the member list (C0), and only the active signer's device signs one (D18). So in an open garden the signer's device admits a person as soon as their request reaches it, with nobody deciding, and a request that reaches only another member's device waits and says so. Whether an open garden also needs a way in when the signer's device cannot be reached, such as an invitation signed in advance, is open (O19). Today's rules on who may change a role hold: nobody removes their own Owner or Steward role one at a time, only an owner changes an owner's roles or removes a Steward role, and the last owner stays an owner. Each role change carries its decider's own signature, so that every device can check the rule without taking the signer's word, and an owner-only change without that owner's signature is refused. How the list carries it is designed here with C0. Exit: E37.
- [ ] **F7 — Money and certificates, settled when connected.** (D40) A cookie-jar claim, a voucher award, a vault action, a support allocation, or bundling approved work into a certificate is prepared and approved locally as a signed intention, under today's rules and limits. A certificate takes approved work only, checked when it is prepared and again at settlement. Nothing moves until a device connects and the right person confirms. At settlement the device rechecks the current authority, limits and balances, and a settlement interrupted by a lost response still happens exactly once. That has to hold at the chain, not only on the device: each intention is bound to something its execution uses up, the settling account's own nonce or a key the contract records, so two devices that submit the same intention at the same moment cannot both settle it, and the authority, cap, balance and budget rules are the contract's. An intention names everything that will execute, not only its amount and purpose: the kind of action, the chain, the contract, the recipient and every parameter. The settlement is built from the intention and nothing else, the person confirming sees those same values, and where a contract accepts the intention's signature, that signature covers all of them with domain separation, so it cannot settle as another action, against another contract or on another chain. Money moves at the chain's time: a claim period, a cap and a balance are judged when the intention settles, under the contract's own rules, and D54 does not change that. Balances shown locally are last-known and say so. Design the intention's format and who may confirm each kind (O14). Exit: E38.
- [ ] **C10 — Direct sync between open devices.** Next lane (D42), ahead of the hosted mailbox. Devices that are open at the same time, on the same network or reachable through a relay, exchange the same signed, encrypted files without anyone carrying them. It needs a signalling service and often a relay, and both devices awake. Carried files stay the fallback. Exit: the direct-transfer cases of E19.
