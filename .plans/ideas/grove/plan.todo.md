# Grove: implementation sequence

**Status:** idea-stage product. Nothing below is built except the disposable G0 spike, and no step is complete. **Created:** 2026-10-11. **Updated:** 2026-11-14.
**MVP contract and decision log:** [spec.md](spec.md).
**Acceptance gates:** [eval.md](eval.md).

Each unchecked step is a future, independently verifiable checkpoint. Checkpoint IDs are stable: a step that moved keeps its ID, and the 18 earlier S/P/K IDs are preserved below. Proposed paths are not existing APIs. Split checkpoints into bounded changes after inspection; do not duplicate storage or capture work.

**Framing, corrected 14 November (D31 to D33).** This sequence makes Green Goods work locally on a phone, in a browser and with an extension. The C checkpoints build the local environment that the app and the extension share. The S, P and K checkpoints build Grove, the extension. A local garden is the unit throughout; where a step says "community" it means a garden's people. The F checkpoints, added the same day, put Green Goods' own functions on that environment, in the gardener app and the steward cockpit (D37 to D44).

## MVP order

The order follows three rules. The first is the owner's: "Local identity and key custody come first." The second is that the cheapest experiment able to change the design runs before anything is built on it. The third is the owner's order for the functions: the Green Goods chain, then promises, then joining and roles, then money and certificates (D39).

The MVP is the first version, and in it every current function works locally, in the gardener app and in the steward cockpit (D37). Each block is usable once its own gate passes.

**Next lanes, in the order they are likely to be needed:** direct sync between devices that are open at the same time (C10, D42); a hosted mailbox with Solid as the preferred adapter (C1a, C3); the Admin Hub snapshot and digest (S7, S9); KOI exchange (K1, K2); co-editing (C5); outside accounts and publication (C6); the wider scope of Milestone B (S10 to S12).

**Research, outside the MVP's gates:** EDV, Automerge + Keyhive (E25), MLS, Bluetooth sensors.

## Community-sharing delivery order

[The contract](community-sharing.md) owns the design; [the research](sharing-research.md) records what is unproven. This plan authorizes no implementation, dependency installation, tracker change or deployment. The entries are listed in the MVP order above, then the later lanes.

- [ ] **C0b — Key and capture durability on real devices.** New on 14 November (R5). On one physical phone and one physical laptop, with no protocol code: create a key pair and a few captures in the Green Goods app installed to the Home Screen, in the same app in a browser tab, and in the packaged extension with a pinned ID (R12). Restart offline. Request persistent storage and record whether it is granted. Leave the app on the phone unopened for fourteen days, and read it once after eight: that is past Safari's seven-day limit and early enough to change the design. Clear site data. Reinstall the extension. Exit: E26. The result decides which phones may hold keys (R13). If the installed app cannot keep keys on a phone, the phone becomes a capture-only device that encrypts to the member's laptop key, and the plan changes before C0.
- [ ] **C0a — Passkey unlock, offline unlock and recovery on two devices.** A gate since 14 November (D19). Run [Pass A](collaboration-passkey-spike.md#pass-a-existing-passkey-offline-unlock-and-recovery) on real existing credentials from both populations: the `greengoods.app` credentials and the older per-site ones. Preserve the credential, its relying-party ID and the account recipe. Test the PRF secret on each passkey provider in use, the extension's route through host permission, a blocked network, a missing or refused prompt, a lost device, a lost credential, a corrupt export and a removed device whose passkey has synced back. Exit: E24. A provider that returns no secret gets the passphrase path; that passes the design and fails passkey unlock for that provider. No account migration, no new credential and no transaction.
- [ ] **C1 — Encrypted vault and shared files.** Choose between age v1 and HPKE inside a named, reviewed multi-recipient envelope, on what the supported devices can run (R1, O3), and record the library, version, license and any audit. Define the signed manifest, the wraps of the vault key, how device private keys are wrapped under it when they are created and imported after unlock, and capture while locked (R15). State one at-rest envelope for a capture, the same whether the vault was locked or unlocked when it was made, and say when, if ever, a locked capture is rewrapped. Name the passphrase derivation, its salt and its cost, and measure it on the supported phone and laptop. Produce test vectors. Prove that a device that was not named cannot open a file; that a tampered, replayed or re-addressed file is rejected; that a copied browser profile without the unlock secret cannot open the vault; that no stored key can sign or decrypt while the vault is locked; and that no file name or content identifier of a private record reaches telemetry (R7). Exit: E16. No private pilot before this gate.
- [ ] **C0 — Identity, pairing, the member list and recovery.** Define the member list as one signed, hash-chained history with one active signer (R3, D18) and no identifier scheme beyond public keys (R2). Prove enrollment with no provider or wallet; pairing by scanned code and a spoken comparison string; a forged member or device entry; removal; the seven-day limit, a limit a community has changed, how an unchanged list stays fresh, and how a community whose devices are all stale shares again (D24); a second controller, its takeover when the first controller's devices and code are lost, and refusal of a fork with a stated way back to one head (O6); the recovery code appointing a new controller device; and separate device keys per community (W3). Exit: E15 and the local cases of E23. Synthetic fixtures only.
- [ ] **C2 — Capture → review → share offline, in the app and in Grove.** Adapt the existing revision and command design for local workspace identifiers and encrypted payloads. Build work reports and promises, through the existing screens and free-form (D34); note, photo, file and link capture in the app and in Grove, and page capture in Grove; manual personal review; the author's share approval, signed by the author's device and bound to a digest of the exact content (D17, R6); file export and import; the recipient's checks and signed receipt; review decisions and corrections; the author's fresh approval before a corrected version leaves; and a steward-approved partner summary as a new projection, carrying the steward's signed approval. Move captures from phone to laptop by file (D21). Use one work report, one promise and one knowledge source, and take each capture type through the loop once. No model, wallet, account or network. Exit: E17, E2 and the applicable parts of E1, E3 and E14, with no false Work, WorkApproval or publication state. Coordinate with S2 to S4, S6 and S8 instead of creating another store.
- [ ] **C4 — Phone boundary and device list.** Reduced on 14 November: direct transfer between devices is its own next lane (C10) and no longer part of this step. Record the exact browsers and devices that pass, what phone suspension does to an export or import in progress, file size and retry limits, how the share sheet and file import behave on iOS and Android, and power use. Exit: E19 for the file carrier. No mesh or background-availability claim. Sensors remain import fixtures until separately scoped.
- [ ] **C1a — Hosted mailbox: the store interface, then Solid.** A later lane, after direct sync (D21, D42). Define the store as opaque objects with put, get, list and delete by an unguessable name, and prove it first with any plain folder. Then follow [the two-device storage contract](storage-comparison-spike.md) on Solid with client-side encryption and local keys. Prove recovery, scoped access, host privacy with the operator's full view, migration and interruptions; record reused and new code, services, licenses and bundle cost. Exit: E22 and the applicable parts of E15 to E19. EDV stays an optional research variant. A failing privacy, permission or recovery gate blocks private use of that adapter; file exchange remains.
- [ ] **C3 — Solid adapter in the clients.** Next lane, after C1a. Pin provider, client and auth versions; prove sign-in and redirect in the extension and the Green Goods app, CORS and CSP, token recovery and suspension. Store ciphertext, key wraps and manifests only; reconcile revisions and receipts; change provider without changing local identity; test the operator's full access with storage permissions bypassed. Exit: E18 and E22. A Pod sign-in never becomes identity or key authority.
- [ ] **C5 — Co-editing with Yjs; Automerge + Keyhive research.** Later lane, opened when two people must edit one draft at once (D16). Use the Yjs core for merging, one new document per audience, with every key holder able to write (R9). Prove encrypted updates, offline edits, restart and reconnect, removed and stale members, history and presence privacy, and recovery on a physical phone and laptop. Exit: E20; a server that can read the document fails it. [Pass B](collaboration-passkey-spike.md#pass-b-yjs-versus-automerge--keyhive) and E25 are backlog research and gate nothing (R11). MLS is a separate, conditional evaluation.
- [ ] **C6 — Outside accounts and publication.** Later lane. Prove private, separately approved associations with a garden or a service account. Verify EOAs and ERC-1271 accounts and the current garden role, and prove a garden link through a Hat-wearing account (R8). Record a release approval before anything is handed to an upload queue, bound to a digest of the exact payload (R6). Test Certified's EOA-only limit instead of claiming GardenAccount support. Scope ATProto/Certified, Ethereum and archival adapters one at a time with their owning projects. Exit: E21 and the outside-account cases of E23; no chain transaction or public upload is authorized by this plan.

**First complete demonstration:** a steward starts a garden with no wallet, defines two Actions and writes the assessment in the cockpit's local build → a gardener reports work on a phone, once through the work screen and once in their own words → pairs their laptop and carries the reports across as a file → on the laptop, Grove's agent proposes the structured version of the free-form report beside the app's screen, and the gardener accepts it → the gardener shares both reports with the steward → the steward approves one and rejects the other with feedback → the decisions return to the gardener's phone → the steward sends a separately approved report to a named funder. Then a promise is made, taken up and confirmed; a second gardener joins; and a cookie-jar claim is approved offline and settles only once a device connects. Alongside, the agent brings in a funding call that fits the garden's assessment while the gardener browses, and shows why it was brought in, where it came from and what to check before sharing. Nothing is uploaded, hosted or signed in to until that claim settles.

## Function blocks

Added 14 November (D37 to D44). These checkpoints put Green Goods' own functions on the loop the C checkpoints prove. Each one runs in the local builds of the gardener app and the steward cockpit, keeps today's rules, and uses today's screens. None of them needs a connection, and none publishes anything.

- [ ] **C7 — Separate local builds of the app and the cockpit.** (D37, D44) Publish the gardener app and the steward cockpit as local builds at their own addresses under `greengoods.app`: the same screens, no analytics and no hosted calls. The existing passkey reaches them through the shared domain (W2). Prove by inspecting the network that a local build makes no request carrying a private record, a file name or a content identifier, that it makes none at all once installed and offline, and that the existing public upload queue cannot be reached from it. Settle the exact addresses and where the module shared with Grove lives (O2). Exit: E30. O8, what limits a code update, still gates sensitive records.
- [ ] **C8 — Start a garden locally, or bring an existing one local.** (D33, D38) A steward starts a garden with no chain: its name, place and first roles become the first signed member list (C0). For a garden that already exists onchain, one connected read brings its public roles, Actions and current assessment onto a steward's device. That steward first proves they wear the garden's steward or owner Hat, with a fresh role check (R8), then signs what was read into the local member list. Other members receive it by file, and the garden works offline from then on. Exit: E31. The read is public data only and brings no work history; the Hub snapshot stays S7.
- [ ] **C9 — Bridge between the local builds and Grove.** (D41) A content script limited to the exact addresses of the local builds relays a typed bridge between the page and Grove. The app or the cockpit passes the record under review. Grove returns the structured version, the agent's assessment and insights, and they are shown beside the app's own screen. Check the sender, the origin, the top frame and the message shape at every boundary. Grove never receives keys, and the page never receives Grove's knowledge captures unless the person shares them. It reuses the relay designed for S7. Exit: E32. Which side holds the garden's keys and records on a laptop is designed here (O17).
- [ ] **F1 — Actions and the assessment.** (D39, D43) In the cockpit's local build, a steward defines the Actions gardeners can report, and a steward or evaluator writes the assessment: baseline, method, outcome targets, selected Actions and reporting period, as today. Both are garden records, signed by their author's device and shared by file. The current assessment is the agent's focus, with any extra topics a steward adds. Exit: E33.
- [ ] **F2 — Work.** (D34) The existing work screen runs in the local build against the garden's local Action list, beside the free-form path that C2 proves. A gardener follows each report on their own device: saved, shared, in review, approved or rejected, with the steward's feedback. Exit: E17 and the status cases of E34.
- [ ] **F3 — Approval.** A steward approves or rejects a work report in the app or the cockpit, with feedback, confidence and verification method, as today. The decision is signed by the steward's device, nobody approves their own work, and the decision returns to the gardener by file. Exit: E34.
- [ ] **F4 — The report for a funder.** At the end of a period a steward builds a report from approved work reports and the assessment's targets, approves it as a release (D17) and sends it to a named funder or partner as an encrypted file. The steward's approval is enough to include an approved work report (D45). The funder reads it without receiving the evidence under it. Exit: E35.
- [ ] **F5 — Promises.** Offers and asks, take-up, proof or linked work, and confirmation, as signed decisions among the garden's devices, through the existing screens or free-form (D34). A steward sets up and seeds the pool and its seasons and campaigns in the cockpit. Today's rules hold: a provider or contributor cannot confirm their own promise, a confirmation cannot be taken back, and the commitment limit applies. Exit: E36.
- [ ] **F6 — Joining and roles.** A person joins an open garden directly, or asks and waits for a steward to accept. A steward grants and revokes roles. Each change is a new version of the member list (C0). Exit: E37.
- [ ] **F7 — Money and certificates, settled when connected.** (D40) A cookie-jar claim, a voucher award, a vault action, or bundling approved work into a certificate is prepared and approved locally as a signed intention, under today's rules and limits. Nothing moves until a device connects and the right person confirms. Balances shown locally are last-known and say so. Design the intention's format and who may confirm each kind (O14). Exit: E38.
- [ ] **C10 — Direct sync between open devices.** Next lane (D42), ahead of the hosted mailbox. Devices that are open at the same time, on the same network or reachable through a relay, exchange the same signed, encrypted files without anyone carrying them. It needs a signalling service and often a relay, and both devices awake. Carried files stay the fallback. Exit: the direct-transfer cases of E19.

## Before execution

The MVP order above governs. Do not put a live model, a Hub connection or a KOI peer ahead of local identity, encryption and manual capture and review.

Confirm the bounded build and its dependencies, select an isolated implementation checkout without changing another session's branch, and settle two open questions first: where local operation of the Green Goods app and the shared core live (O2), and whether any Coop code may be copied (O5). This planning request is not an instruction to install dependencies or start product code.
