**Updated 10 October 2026 UTC.** The user has accepted the four pillars, the overall narrative sequence, and the cover typography, weight, accent and copy. Green Goods and its global mission lead the pitch. Final copy, first cohort, pricing and ask remain proposals. This document supersedes the OS-first framing and its unvalidated commercial numbers. ## Proposition and vision

User-supplied proposition, retained as the authority:

> Green Goods helps communities caring for land and each other access useful tools at a low cost, learn from one another and coordinate support while retaining sovereignty over their knowledge.

Suggested refinement:

> Green Goods helps communities caring for land and each other access affordable tools and infrastructure, learn from one another and coordinate support while retaining sovereignty over their knowledge.

“Affordable” connects cost to people's circumstances; “infrastructure” makes the practical systems explicit. This is a value proposition to deliver and test, not measured affordability today. Support includes time, skills, relationships, equipment and funding. Sovereignty means effective community authority, permissions, attribution, portability, recovery and exit. It does not promise exclusive ownership of every contribution or control over copies already disclosed.

Proposed vision:

> A world where every community, regardless of economic circumstances, can access the tools, infrastructure, knowledge and support to care for land and each other.

Global ambition requires local delivery: languages, connectivity, disability access, land relationships and community governance shape participation. A first cohort must not turn the mission into a product only for wealthy users or one country. Supported access and noncash contributions need sustainable delivery costs and a named payer; cross-subsidy is not automatic.

Green Goods is the product, protocol and ecosystem. The proposed OS is its accessible workspace. Explain the whole offer as **tools and infrastructure; shared learning and relationships; accountable work and coordinated support**. Distinguish current capabilities from proposed extensions and later experiments. Hardware packages, membership and every protocol flow are not established as available today.

## Agreed pillars

| Pillar | Observable commitment |
|---|---|
| Accessibility | Participation fits people's budgets, devices, connectivity, languages, abilities, skills and time. A useful manual path remains available. |
| Sovereignty | People and communities exercise legitimate authority over knowledge, permissions, governance, portability, recovery and exit. |
| Trust | Sources, uncertainty, review and correction are understandable; relationships and delivery are accountable. |
| Reciprocity | Contributors receive useful learning, skills, relationships or resources and can decline sharing without surrendering their own records. |

Privacy, Interoperability and Verifiability are essential throughout. Affordability supports access, defined rights support sovereignty, and appropriate transparency supports trust. Transparency never requires sensitive disclosure. Verifying a source or signature does not establish an environmental outcome. These are accepted strategic principles, not certification of the current product.

## People, first user and buyer

Use **land stewards** as the umbrella. Keep farmers in two examples because they make the work concrete:

1. A mid-sized farmer transitioning toward regenerative practices.
2. A small regenerative farmer maintaining a viable livelihood.
3. A community managing land in the Global South, with its own authority, expertise and priorities.
4. A well-resourced land steward investing in restoration and practical infrastructure.

These are settings to investigate, not validated market segments or a hierarchy of expertise. Constraints exist everywhere. Every setting can contribute and receive; wealth does not determine the direction of knowledge transfer.

**Proposed entry point:** one locally led stewardship cohort supported by a trusted coordinator. Primary users are participating land stewards; the coordinator supports onboarding, review and useful exchange. The buyer hypothesis is the delivery organization's program or operations lead. A separate sponsor may fund access, with no automatic right to community records. A farmer/adviser cohort remains a concrete candidate. Geography, purchasing authority, budget and one repeated local question still require discovery. The earlier U.S. conservation-district count does not select the market.

## Evidence and production gates

The previous subscription price, fit percentage, ARR, margins and funding ask were authored assumptions. They are withdrawn from the next core pitch. [market-sizing.md](market-sizing.md) remains historical sensitivity analysis. Market size requires an agreed buyer, geography, denominator, qualification and delivery capacity. A funding ask requires a scoped workplan and reviewed costs. A large ecological need is not software revenue.

The contribution to test is continuity between useful private stewardship knowledge, voluntary reciprocal learning, community review and accountable support. OpenTEAM and GainForest/Taina overlap significantly. Ma Earth and Hypercerts already work on portability and funding records. Integration and contribution are substantive options.

Use the [website design references](artifacts/design-reference.md): Fraunces/Inter, warm linen, charcoal, restrained green and contextual photography. Cover typography decided 9 October 2026 Pacific, 10 October UTC: Fraunces Medium 500 upright for titles with no italic, charcoal on linen with one phrase in the website's accent green, Inter for supporting copy; the website's italic stays a website device. The cover subtitle is the 18-word land-stewards line in the slide table; "infrastructure" moves to the solution slide and notes. New imagery must tell the story. Initial briefs should cover care in a specific place, exchange around practical work and a clear proposed workspace experience. Website assets establish visual language; their documentary provenance is not assumed. Generated places and activities remain conceptual. No generated human faces or heads, including background figures, photographs, screens and reflections. Prefer places, tools, infrastructure and cropped hands. Text belongs on the left and imagery on the right of split layouts. Keep the full proposition in notes and the cover hook short. Avoid rejected gouache and stereotyped wealth/poverty contrasts.
