# Strategy and theory of change

**Stage 3 · 8 October 2026 · independent strategic assessment, with no partner commitments inferred.** This brief follows the [research and PRD reviews](eval.md). Sources resolve in the [evidence register](evidence-register.md); all commercial scenarios are illustrative assumptions.

**Current direction:** the user has accepted the four pillars and overall sequence in [pitch-revision.md](pitch-revision.md). Green Goods leads as the product, protocol and ecosystem; the OS is a proposed workspace. The vision is global access regardless of economic circumstances, with land stewards as the umbrella and four concrete settings. Ma Earth, Hypercerts, 5th World and The BioFi Project are intended pitch audiences, not confirmed partners. See the [audience research](evidence-register.md#partner-audience-research).

Recommend starting with one locally led cohort and a trusted coordinator. A farmer/adviser setting remains a candidate, with the delivery organization's program lead as a possible buyer. Geography, workflow, purchasing authority and budget remain unresolved. The four pillars are Accessibility, Sovereignty, Trust and Reciprocity; Privacy, Interoperability and Verifiability remain essential throughout.

**Commercial evidence correction:** all prices, margins, cohort forecasts, market shares and budget envelopes below are historical scenario assumptions. They are withdrawn from the next core pitch pending reviewed inputs. The [earlier market model](market-sizing.md) is not an adopted pricing strategy, market selection or funding ask. Keep services and recurring support as the first revenue hypothesis; test membership, infrastructure, education, protocol fees and licensing separately. Global accessibility needs a funded delivery model, not assumed cross-subsidy.

The strongest objection is that a field form, shared folder and capable facilitator might solve the problem more cheaply. The pilot must compare that baseline, plus ODK/Kobo or farmOS where appropriate. Existing local-AI, community knowledge and AT projects, including GainForest/Taina, also weaken novelty claims. Green Goods should integrate or contribute where those tools already solve the job. Its credible advantage is the possibility of continuity into its own community review/protocol, not ownership of every layer. W20–27; R03–10.

## Causal chain, tests and attribution

| Step | Proposed mechanism | Assumptions / failure modes | Measurable test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accessible tools → participation | Manual/offline participation lowers the practical cost of recording | Available device/power/language support; setup, disability barriers or sponsor reporting can still exclude people | Completion and dropout by device/language/role; data/setup cost; assisted vs independent completion; include nonparticipants |
| Participation → useful local memory | Source-linked records preserve context across shifts and seasons | Correct inputs, time/units and trusted correction process; collecting more data can create noise or surveillance | Weekly successful retrieval, handover questions answered, correction rate, missed/duplicate tasks; baseline comparison |
| Local memory → better decisions | Steward sees missing/unusual readings and checks the right place | Records concern a real choice; model summaries may add error or labor | Counterbalanced manual/assisted task, reviewer time, justified decisions and avoidable rework; agronomist review if prescriptions ever enter scope |
| Useful memory → reciprocal exchange | Communities share selected lessons and receive situated feedback | Matching contexts, translation/curation and trust; wealthy participants can dominate or extract | Bidirectional useful reuse, contributor benefit, questions answered, attribution retained, refusal respected; record negative transfers |
| Better coordination → accountable commitments | Agreed responsibilities can be linked to reviewed work and fulfillment decisions | Role legitimacy, fair dispute/review process and reliable product flow; metrics can become performative | Completion/dispute/rework rates and perceived fairness; later compare similar commitments, not token volume |
| Accountable work → possible capital formation | Supporters can assess a legible record of action and limitations | A real funder budget and adequate methodology; evidence alone does not create demand or legal eligibility | Cost/time to diligence, actual repeat support and reasons for rejection; distinguish pledges from received funds |
| Capital/support → durable stewardship | Funding buys labor, maintenance and learning that improve land care | Reliable delivery and community control; market, weather, tenure and governance confound results | Retention of capacity, maintenance completion and separately designed ecological measures over an appropriate time horizon |

This is a testable theory, not a proven feedback loop. Eight weeks can examine usability, effort, relationships and limited operational choices. It cannot establish long-term regeneration or causal financial returns. Weather, soil, management history, staffing, land tenure and outside funding may explain outcomes. Use baseline/time-series records, matched tasks and qualitative accounts; predefine metrics, include failures and record attribution uncertainty. Do not invent a yield-to-impact conversion from dollars to biodiversity or culture. R15; L02–04.

## Multiple forms of value

| Value | Example | How to recognize it without making it interchangeable |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | Paid steward time, lower rework, reliable support, received community funding | Cash received/cost avoided with boundaries and evidence; distinguish community funds from company revenue |
| Social | A trusted peer to ask, better handovers, fair review | Reciprocal relationships, response quality, conflict resolution and participant accounts |
| Intellectual | Reusable method, source-linked lesson, calibrated dataset | Attributed reuse and corrections; rights and local applicability travel with the contribution |
| Cultural | Continuity of local knowledge, language and stewardship practices | Community-chosen accounts and permissions; a right to remain private or unmodeled |
| Experiential | Confidence, practical skill, learning from a failed experiment | Demonstrated capability, reflection and mentorship; no automatic monetary or token conversion |

No common score is necessary. A community can value a teaching relationship even when it produces no public dataset or revenue. Preserve differences in practice instead of ranking bioregions using a universal leaderboard.

## Protocol contribution and limits

Commitment pooling can make agreed work, contributors, evidence and review responsibilities explicit. The inspected lifecycle has creation, claim/acceptance, evidence/work links, readiness/confirmation and dispute/terminal paths. `openCycle` takes allocation and recognition policy; `closeCycle` locks the fulfilled set before later certificate operations. The registry tracks non-transferable commitment units and provider capacity. It is not a generic liquid token ledger or proof that a promise is financially redeemable. R11.

This could later support commitments such as maintaining a sensor, teaching a calibration procedure or delivering a field inspection round. It helps only if the parties agree on units, completion evidence, authority and appeal. Do not force every informal exchange into a contract. Pooling's capability ledger remains deployed-not-available, and exchange architecture/compatibility work is gated; no cross-community settlement liquidity, voucher market or automatic currency backing should be sold from this proposal. R03/R12.

Endowment/vault mechanisms may support recurring stewardship from contributed capital and actual realized strategy returns. The inspected Octant module reports strategy profit/loss and registers donation shares; the Yield resolver redeems available shares and routes funds according to configured destinations. These are distinct from the client campaign-vault path, whose inspected redemption hook explicitly requires Ethereum mainnet and a connected owning wallet. Do not infer every Green Goods chain/account mode works for every vault. R13–14.

The share receiver and owner matter: an endowment transaction can allocate rights differently from a simple donation. Explain fees, withdrawal availability, max-loss settings, smart-contract/strategy/asset/market risks and administrative controls before participation. No principal or yield guarantee. An illustrative $100,000 × 3% gross annual yield equals $3,000 before losses/fees; that arithmetic is not a forecast and cannot fund a $30,000 pilot on its own. Community yield routing is not automatically Green Goods operating revenue. Neither model output nor a sensor should initiate financial actions.

At a purely illustrative $60,000 annual fixed operating/R&D cost, remaining result is −$60,480 / −$40,900 / +$17,750. Even the base case is not self-sustaining at that cost. ## Bootstrapping reciprocity and knowledge governance

Begin with a small facilitated cohort around the same operational question, then invite one carefully chosen cross-bioregion comparison. Before sharing a lesson, compare soil/media, rainfall, crop or planting stage, labor and equipment constraints. A method that helps one place may harm another. Retrieval should display applicability, source date, limitations, attribution and whether the contributor permits adaptation or further sharing.

Pay for curation/translation and recognize noncash work, including corrections and explaining why a practice does not transfer. Track contributor benefit, not just uploads or likes. A well-resourced participant can offer expertise, equipment support or paid facilitation; a resource-constrained community can offer valued contextual knowledge without being required to expose sensitive information. Neither group's benefit should be assumed from participation alone.

Community representatives should set collection/publication rules, appoint and rotate reviewers, disclose conflicts and hear appeals. Separate service-provider administration from authority over knowledge. Provide tiered audiences, purpose limits where enforceable, attribution preferences, takedown/correction processes and an exit/export path. Publicly licensed materials must explain actual onward-sharing rights; private agreements cannot guarantee control over already public copies. Do not make access contingent on AI training consent.

The pilot should count successful exchanges in each direction, follow-up conversations, justified adaptations, inappropriate recommendations, contributor satisfaction and review/translation effort. Stop expanding the network if curation costs exceed benefit, wealth determines whose practice is called authoritative, or people feel pressured to publish.

## Proposed ask and sequencing

Ask for **up to US$12,000 and two weeks** to test the narrow prototype, with a product owner, technical lead and paid community/language reviewer assigned. This is a proposed authorization, not a request to spend in this task. Use the itemized research budget. Release implementation only after G1–G4 prerequisites appropriate to synthetic/export/sandbox work are satisfied; do not require a license transition to begin.

Then decide separately on **up to US$30,000 for an eight-week, three-community pilot**, conditional on privacy/recovery proof, useful task completion, actual community consent and a credible budget owner. Real sensing or external inference requires its own gate. Require a paid continuation experiment, measured cost/review effort and bidirectional usefulness before adding a managed sync service or more workflows.

The immediate strategic decision is whether to sponsor this bounded learning effort. The long-term network, membership and financial model should be earned through evidence. If the simpler baseline wins, Green Goods can still contribute portable evidence and protocol adapters without becoming an all-purpose OS.
