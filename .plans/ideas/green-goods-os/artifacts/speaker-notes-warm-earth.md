## 01. Green Goods OS — Local knowledge. Shared regeneration.

Green Goods OS is a proposed community workspace for collecting, understanding, curating, controlling and selectively sharing knowledge about land, activities and impact. The ambition is local usefulness that can become reciprocal learning across places. OS describes coordinated knowledge and services, not a new device operating system. Green Goods remains the product/protocol/ecosystem; Coop is a separate reference project. Cover artwork is an AI-generated fictional landscape, not evidence of a real pilot or ecological outcome.

## 08. Four principles that make the product worth using

Proposed framework, subject to community evaluation: accessibility across cost, existing hardware, bandwidth, language, disability, skill and time; sovereignty through permissions, identity, governance, portability, recovery and exit; trust through source provenance, human review, limitations and reliable delivery; reciprocity through benefits to contributors as well as recipients. Privacy, interoperability and verifiability are essential across all four. Tradeoffs include more replication increasing exposure, stronger controls adding work, and transparency conflicting with sensitive knowledge. Ownership language must not imply exclusive legal rights over every observation. Trust comes from relationships and reliable practice, not signatures alone.

## 09. A lesson should help the place it came from, too

Reciprocal network hypothesis: people in different bioregions can benefit from situated practical experience, expertise and resources. Better-resourced stewards can fund curation, equipment support or facilitation and receive useful contextual knowledge. Resource-constrained communities may contribute valued practice and receive expertise, peer feedback and support, without pressure to disclose. These roles are not fixed and neither group automatically knows more. Attribution, consent, language, applicability and different practices travel with the lesson. The pilot measures useful exchanges in both directions and contributor benefit, not upload counts. Knowledge, cultural continuity, experience and relationships need not be converted into tokens.

## 16. Useful knowledge can support collective action

Theory of change: accessible tools can support local memory; useful records and trusted relationships may enable reciprocal learning and better choices; agreed work can become more accountable; credible records may help supporters assess commitments. Each link is conditional. Intellectual, cultural, experiential, social and financial value are distinct, not interchangeable tokens. Green Goods commitment pooling can associate responsibilities, evidence and reviewed fulfillment, with non-transferable units/capacity and gated product availability. Endowment/vault mechanisms may route actual realized returns to ongoing work, subject to strategy, asset, smart-contract, administrative and liquidity risks. ## 20. Private collaboration and public AT are separate

Private collaborative state can use a CRDT such as Yjs or Automerge, with explicit authorization, encrypted storage, conflict presentation and backups. atcute is an AT client/toolkit candidate; libp2p may help peer connectivity but still needs signaling, NAT traversal/relays and lifecycle recovery. Neither turns browser instances into an always-available authoritative PDS. Use a persistent public service and coordinated publisher; never give every writer a shared repository signing key. Public AT repositories stay public when self-hosted; permissioned-data proposals are experimental. Revocation cannot recover disclosed copies. Exact approved bytes, identity and destination bind publication. Draft/model/sensor input is untrusted, prompt injection cannot grant tool authority, and models never authorize chain transactions. Offline retries must reconcile prior results before reissuing.
