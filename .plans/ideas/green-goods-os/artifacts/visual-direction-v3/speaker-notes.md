## 1. Cover, Fraunces Medium 500 with the accent green second line

Green Goods helps communities caring for land and each other access useful tools at a low cost, learn from one another and coordinate support while retaining sovereignty over their knowledge.

Our global vision is that every community, regardless of economic circumstances, can access the tools, infrastructure, knowledge and support to care for land and each other. Local language, governance, ability, connectivity and time matter alongside money. Green Goods is the product, protocol and ecosystem. The proposed OS is a workspace within that wider offer.

Affordability and practical sovereignty are commitments to deliver and test. They are not measured savings or a promise of control over copies already shared. The four agreed pillars are Accessibility, Sovereignty, Trust and Reciprocity, with Privacy, Interoperability and Verifiability essential throughout.

## 2. Solution sample: A browser workspace for land stewardship

Green Goods brings practical tools, shared learning and coordinated support into one product and ecosystem. The proposed OS is an accessible browser workspace, with optional capture, local assistance and bounded sensor/import integrations. Manual participation must remain useful without new hardware, AI or public sharing.

The intended participant experience is to keep a useful observation, find and review its context, decide what to share, learn from another community and coordinate needed support. Financial, social, intellectual, cultural and experiential value can all matter. These forms of value are not automatically interchangeable or tokenized.

Current foundations: the inspected Green Goods capability ledger and work modules describe Garden records, work capture/review and support paths with availability limits. Proposed extensions: the connected OS workspace, source-linked local assistance, deliberate knowledge exchange and compatible infrastructure services. Later gated work: broader mesh behavior and expanded protocol integration. The commitment-pooling ledger distinguishes deployed infrastructure from an available product flow. No guarantee of ecological outcome, principal, yield or funding follows.

Sovereignty means meaningful permissions, community authority, portability, recovery and exit. The sharing decision must show exactly what leaves the private workspace. Supporters receive only deliberately shared material; their funding does not confer ownership or AI-training permission.
