## 1. Land care within reach

Green Goods helps communities caring for land and each other access useful tools at a low cost, learn from one another and coordinate support while retaining sovereignty over their knowledge.
Our global vision is that every community, regardless of economic circumstances, can access the tools, infrastructure, knowledge and support to care for land and each other. Local language, governance, ability, connectivity and time matter alongside money. Green Goods is the product, protocol and ecosystem. The proposed OS is a workspace within that wider offer.
Affordability and practical sovereignty are commitments to deliver and test. They are not measured savings or a promise of control over copies already shared. The four agreed pillars are Accessibility, Sovereignty, Trust and Reciprocity, with Privacy, Interoperability and Verifiability essential throughout.

## 2. Unequal access makes regenerative work harder

Cost, complex setup and knowledge silos can leave communities spending scarce time managing systems. Knowledge silos leave useful experience scattered across tools and communities, making it harder to find, carry forward and adapt elsewhere.
FAO's Near East and North Africa regional overview identifies affordability, connectivity, skills, infrastructure and fragmented digital-solution markets as barriers. This supports investigating these mechanisms; it does not quantify their prevalence among Green Goods users or establish a global rate. The consequences shown are a problem hypothesis for participant discovery, not customer testimony or observed product savings.
Different tools are not inherently a problem. The concern is where inaccessible services, incompatible records or missing context create avoidable effort. Existing notebooks, conversations and community practices may work well and should remain part of the comparison.
Source, accessed 9 October 2026 UTC: https://www.fao.org/neareast/main-topics/action-areas/digital-transformation/ (P07 in the evidence register). No financial, market-size or impact statistic is asserted.

## 4. A lesson learned in one place can help another.

Shared knowledge should preserve local context, attribution, consent and differences, and help relationships grow within and across bioregions. A lesson travels as a record with its context, a question elsewhere finds it by choice, and feedback returns to the contributor, including what did not transfer.
Reciprocal value is a hypothesis. The pilot gate requires at least one documented useful exchange in each direction for each matched pair. Curation, translation and facilitation are paid work in the plan, not assumed volunteer effort.

## 5. A browser workspace for land stewardship

Green Goods brings practical tools, shared learning and coordinated support into one product and ecosystem. The proposed OS is an accessible browser workspace, with optional capture, local assistance and bounded sensor/import integrations. Manual participation must remain useful without new hardware, AI or public sharing.
The intended participant experience is to keep a useful observation, find and review its context, decide what to share, learn from another community and coordinate needed support. Financial, social, intellectual, cultural and experiential value can all matter. These forms of value are not automatically interchangeable or tokenized.
Current foundations: the inspected Green Goods capability ledger and work modules describe Garden records, work capture/review and support paths with availability limits. Proposed extensions: the connected OS workspace, source-linked local assistance, deliberate knowledge exchange and compatible infrastructure services. Later gated work: broader mesh behavior and expanded protocol integration. The commitment-pooling ledger distinguishes deployed infrastructure from an available product flow. No guarantee of ecological outcome, principal, yield or funding follows.
Sovereignty means meaningful permissions, community authority, portability, recovery and exit. The sharing decision must show exactly what leaves the private workspace. Supporters receive only deliberately shared material; their funding does not confer ownership or AI-training permission.

## 13. Pay for useful services and dependable support.

Recommended sequence: fixed-scope service, then paid support, then a curated knowledge service if reciprocal use exists, then compatible kits through local partners if installation pain is proven. Protocol fees and commercial licensing stay out of the initial revenue model. Each hypothesis has its own payer, demand evidence and validation experiment.
The earlier subscription price, fit percentage, ARR and margins were authored assumptions and are withdrawn from this core pitch. Access funding for communities is distinct from company revenue; a sponsor paying for access does not buy community records.
Source: .plans/ideas/green-goods-os/strategy.md, Revenue hypotheses, evaluated separately, and Illustrative scenarios (historical, withdrawn).

## 14. Grow through trusted delivery and useful exchange.

Growth comes through trusted delivery and useful exchange: a coordinator onboards one cohort, weekly use becomes routine, the buyer renews after a measured pilot, and participants introduce the next cohort. Convert a successful pilot rather than mass advertising. Maintenance ownership is named before anything is installed.
This is a proposed go-to-market, not measured acquisition. No renewal evidence exists yet; the pilot requires at least one buyer willing to sign a defined paid continuation.
Sources: .plans/ideas/green-goods-os/strategy.md, Revenue hypotheses (acquisition paths) and Proposed ask and sequencing; .plans/ideas/green-goods-os/spec.md, Pilot and production gates.

## 15. Prove usefulness, control and reciprocal value.

Exists today, per the inspected capability ledger and modules at the pinned commit: Garden records, work capture and steward review (R03, R06, R07), donations and vault endowments (R13, R14), the installed app with service worker and offline work queue (R04, R06), and attestation schemas with role and time rules (R09). Deployment evidence is dated, not a live check.
Proposed: the private workspace with a disclosure preview, deliberate knowledge exchange, source-linked local assistance and compatible infrastructure services (PRD). Unproven, to test in the pilot: usefulness without added effort, real control through recovery and exit drills, useful exchange in each direction for each matched pair, and a buyer who signs a defined paid continuation.
Pilot: eight weeks, three communities doing the same workflow, ideally two resource-constrained sites and one better-resourced learning peer, with compensated facilitation and translation. Advancement requires zero unresolved severe privacy or recovery defects, at least two communities using the workflow weekly through week six, no net increase in median task effort, and the exchange and buyer thresholds above.
