## Historical market definition

Green Goods OS proposes a supported knowledge workspace for land-management communities. The initial economic buyer is the program director or operations lead of a conservation/advisory organization serving several farms. The first user is a mid-sized farmer transitioning practices, working with a trusted adviser. A program pays for onboarding, recovery, support and facilitated knowledge exchange; members retain useful access and export rights.

For a measurable initial channel, use U.S. conservation districts. This is a recommended commercial discovery geography, not a claim of existing access or a limit on the global mission. Cooperatives, NGOs, private stewards and international networks remain expansion segments; they are not added to the denominator without a deduplicated buyer inventory. A district is a possible purchasing organization, not a farmer, a qualified lead or an assured customer.

## Calculation chain

Proposed annual program price: **$3,000 / $6,000 / $9,000**. Base service scope: up to four community workspaces, shared onboarding materials, capped support, recovery drills and facilitated peer learning. This bundles the earlier $125/community/month assumption: 4 × $125 × 12 = $6,000. It is not an additional fee on top of that community fee. Price and scope require paid validation. Hardware, travel and specialist agronomy are separately scoped.

| Measure | Formula | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| U.S. channel ceiling (bounded segment TAM) | 3,000 districts × annual price | $9m | $18m | $27m |
| Planning SAM | 3,000 × assumed 10% fit × annual price | $0.9m | $1.8m | $2.7m |
| 24-month obtainable target, at base price | 5 / 15 / 30 programs × $6,000 | $30k ARR | $90k ARR | $180k ARR |

The channel ceiling assumes universal purchase and is an upper bound. The SAM's 10% is an unvalidated filter for an active relevant program, staff capacity, compatible procurement and budget; it is not a surveyed addressable share. SOM is a delivery/acquisition target, not a forecast or pipeline. All results exclude onboarding, hardware, community donations, protocol volume, endowment principal, grants and license fees. Do not sum a program's fee with its participating farms as separate revenue.

At the base $6,000 price, a SAM fit rate of 5% / 10% / 20% produces $0.9m / $1.8m / $3.6m. The revenue model is most sensitive to qualified fit, price and support cost. Global opportunity is unquantified; the 608m figure and UNEP finance totals must never be multiplied by a subscription price or called the software TAM.

## Service economics and buyer value tests

At $6,000 recurring revenue, assume 60 support/curation hours × $40 = $2,400; hosting, backup and distribution $600; translation/accessibility reserve $300. Direct annual delivery cost **$3,300**, contribution **$2,700 (45%)** before acquisition, core R&D, administration, tax and general legal/security overhead. At the same scope/cost, $3,000 pricing yields **−$300 (−10%)**, and $9,000 yields **$5,700 (63.3%)**. These are assumptions, not supplier quotes or observed margins. At 120 labor hours, base-price contribution falls to $300 (5%).

Separate onboarding: $2,000 price, $1,400 assumed direct cost, $600 contribution. With $60,000 annual fixed overhead/R&D (illustrative), 15 year-round base-price programs provide $40,500 recurring contribution and still leave a $19,500 gap before acquisition costs. Approximately 23 programs are needed to cover that fixed-cost assumption from recurring contribution alone; real staffing, security and acquisition costs could raise the threshold.

A buyer-value threshold makes the offer falsifiable: $6,000/year divided by a $40/hour loaded staff-cost assumption is **150 staff hours/year**, or **12.5 hours/month**. That is the time saving needed to cover the price if time were the only benefit. It is not a promised saving. Measure record retrieval, repeat data entry, preparation, support and review time together; also assess continuity, safer sharing and usefulness of peer feedback. Crop returns are outside this estimate.

## Go-to-market and evidence priorities

Start with one conservation/advisory program director and three consenting field workspaces. Select for a real recurring question, a named facilitator, an actual budget owner and freedom to refuse sharing. Use public district/association directories for discovery after authorization; no access, interest or procurement qualification is assumed.

The proposed first six-month funnel is 30 qualified buyer conversations → 6 scoped proposals → 3 paid pilots → 2 paid continuations. It is a target, with 20% / 50% / 67% stage conversion assumptions. Log losses and procurement delays. Before scaling, replace the assumptions with budgets, observed support hours, a baseline comparison and renewals. A two-year base target of 15 active programs needs repeatable referrals, multiple acquisition cohorts and adequate delivery staff; it does not follow automatically from the first funnel.

Do not assume a government channel can buy quickly. Parallel discovery with cooperatives and NGO programs may reveal a better first buyer, but only one initial delivery package should be built. Five buyer interviews with verifiable budget/procurement details, and a paid continuation, are more valuable now than a larger speculative global market number.

## Evidence review before use in the pitch

**Disposition: usable as a labeled planning model; insufficient for revenue forecasts, valuation or claims of demand.** Checked unit consistency, geographic scope, source dates, duplicate-counting risks, price sensitivity, delivery-cost sensitivity and arithmetic. Kept the global population and nature-finance context outside the revenue calculation. Published-survey associations support the channel hypothesis, not product-market fit. Market size is presented as a bounded channel ceiling, a conditional SAM and an obtainable target; each has a visibly different confidence level.
