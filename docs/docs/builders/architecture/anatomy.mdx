# Anatomy of a Work Submission

The fastest way to understand Green Goods is to follow one piece of work all the way through:
from a phone in a garden with no signal to a verifiable record a funder can check. Every package
appears here in its real role, so by the end you'll know where any change you're planning would
live.

The trace below follows the real thing on Arbitrum One. The schema links open the live registry,
and every stage names the code that owns it.

## 1. Capture

A gardener documents work in the [client PWA](../packages/client): pick the action, capture the
media, review before submitting. The flow controller behind those screens lives in
`packages/shared/src/hooks/client-ui/work/useWorkSubmissionFlowController.ts`. Nothing here
requires connectivity; photos, notes, and the chosen action all stay on the device.

## 2. Queue

Submitting hands the work to the shared job queue (`packages/shared/src/modules/job-queue`),
which saves it on the device. It survives restarts and dead zones, so a gardener without signal
loses nothing.

Once the connection is back, the app gets the queued work ready in the background, for wallet
and passkey users alike (`packages/shared/src/modules/work/upload-preparation.ts`). Sending still
waits for the gardener: they open Your Work and tap **Upload all**, and everything goes up in one
step. Nothing is sent on their behalf.

## 3. Attest

When the gardener taps Upload all, the shared EAS module (`packages/shared/src/modules/data/eas.ts`)
encodes the submission against the
[Green Goods Work Submission schema](https://arbitrum.easscan.org/schema/view/0x43ebd37da5479df9d495a4c6514e7cb7f370e9f4166a0a58e14a3baf466078c4)
and writes the attestation. The write succeeds only if
[`Work.sol`](https://github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods/blob/develop/packages/contracts/src/resolvers/Work.sol)
approves it. The resolver checks the submitter's [hats](../integrations/hats) for that garden:
gardeners can submit, and so can stewards and the garden's owner, because each role includes the
ones below it. An unauthorized submission doesn't become a bad record; it never becomes a record
at all.

## 4. Land in the read paths

Two read paths pick the work up, deliberately separate:

- The **attestation itself** is read through the shared [EAS data layer](../integrations/eas);
  attestations stay out of the Envio indexer on purpose, so the record's truth path is
  independent of ours.
- The **protocol events around it** (garden state, membership, module activity) flow through the
  [indexer](../packages/indexer)'s GraphQL read models.

## 5. Review

A steward opens the [admin cockpit](../packages/admin)'s Garden workspace and sees the submission
with its evidence. Review is a human judgment; the protocol's job was getting the evidence here
intact.

## 6. Approve

The steward's decision becomes its own attestation against the
[Work Approval schema](https://arbitrum.easscan.org/schema/view/0x6f44cac380791858e86c67c75de1f10b186fb6534c00f85b596709a3cd51f381),
referencing the work it judges, with feedback and a confidence score.
[`WorkApproval.sol`](https://github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods/blob/develop/packages/contracts/src/resolvers/WorkApproval.sol)
lets only the garden's stewards or its owner record it, and it rejects anyone approving their own
work. Submission and judgment are two records, by two people, under two authorities; that
separation is the protocol's honesty.

## 7. Display

Status flows back to the gardener through the
[work-display-status lifecycle](./data-model#work-display-status): pending, approved, or rejected
with feedback, plus the syncing states the queue adds on top. The UI rule from the
[Architecture page](../architecture#where-data-lives) applies here: unconfirmed work is never
shown as protocol truth.

## 8. Onward

Approved work is what everything downstream builds on: it feeds the garden's
[assessments](/community/steward-guide/making-an-assessment), can be bundled into a
[hypercert](../integrations/hypercerts), and justifies the flows moving through
[vaults](../integrations/octant) and [jars](../integrations/cookie-jar). The community docs'
[How It Works](/community/how-it-works) tells this same story in product language.
