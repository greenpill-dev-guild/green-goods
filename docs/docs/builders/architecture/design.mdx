# Design

Green Goods has one design system, Warm Earth, and three dialects of it: the installed app, the
public website, and the admin cockpit, with these docs as a quiet fourth. This page starts with the
system everything shares, then shows what each dialect keeps, changes, and forbids, and ends with
how the system is governed in the repo.

## Why the product feels this way {#why-warm-earth}

Regenerative work happens in places, over seasons, and through relationships. The interface
therefore favors natural light, earth tones, legible material layers, calm motion, and language
that names the work plainly. The [ontology](./data-model) supplies the names for entities,
personas, and lifecycle states; the design system decides how those concepts look and move on each
surface. The [generated glossary](/glossary) is the public bridge between the two.

## One system: Warm Earth {#system}

The root [`DESIGN.md`](https://github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods/blob/develop/DESIGN.md)
is the canonical source. Every dialect file extends it, and the shared `theme.css` is only the
runtime projection of its tokens. The concept is a community garden: sun-bleached linen, charcoal
ink, and the green of new growth, handmade but precise. Three qualities steer every decision:
warmth (higher chroma, spring motion), clarity (functional layers, content-forward hierarchy), and
purpose (no engagement hacking, no dark patterns).

### Color by volume {#color}

Color roles are defined by how much of the screen they occupy, not by a palette ranking. Green is
third in volume and first in visual pull, and flooding a screen with it is the system's named
failure mode.

| Role | Volume | Color | Light / dark | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neutral (canvas) | 80 to 90% | Warm linen | `#FAF8F5` / `#1C1917` | Background and breathing room |
| Primary (ink) | 8 to 15% | Warm charcoal | `#292524` / `#F5F5F4` | Headings and body |
| Secondary (stone) | 3 to 5% | Earth stone | `#78716C` / `#A8A29E` | Metadata, borders, labels |
| Tertiary (accent) | 1 to 3% | Garden green | `#1FC16B` | Calls to action, active states, value flow |

Amber (`#D97706`) and sky (`#3B82F6`) are situational accents for warnings and information. A green
fill that carries text uses the contrast-safe action pair, never the bright accent itself.

### Type, shape, motion, material {#foundations}

- **Type.** Inter in the installed app, Plus Jakarta Sans in the admin cockpit, and a serif
  (Fraunces) only for editorial headlines on the public website.
- **Shape.** Buttons take one corner per surface whatever their emphasis: a 12px squircle in the
  app, square on the website, pills in the cockpit. Fields are 16px, and buttons and fields share
  one height scale: 48, 44, 40, and 32px.
- **Motion.** Named spring tokens only, such as `--spring-spatial` and `--spring-effects`. Nothing
  settles on a hand-written curve or duration.
- **Material and elevation.** Five material thicknesses from ultrathin to solid, matched to content
  density, and five elevation layers from the substrate to overlays. Body text never sits on the
  thinnest glass.

## Three dialects {#surfaces}

The dialects share tokens and terminology without pretending every audience has the same needs.
Each has its own DesignMD file, and the hard rule between them is the chrome: a bottom app bar
means the installed app, a site header means the website, and the Material 3 shell means the
cockpit. Never mix them. The [client](../packages/client) and [admin](../packages/admin) package
pages describe the shells that enforce the split.

### The installed app: a field journal {#pwa}

Gardeners install the client as a PWA and use it in the field, often offline. The dialect is
practical and personal: mobile-first and thumb-zone optimized, a bottom `AppBar` with Home,
Garden, and Profile, Inter only (a tool, not a magazine), the 12px squircle on every button, and
warm offline states rather than red ones. Sheets carry most of the work: every bottom sheet names
one of four heights and shares one header, one action bar, and one loading anatomy, so a gardener
never relearns a screen. Its rules live in
[`packages/client/DESIGN.pwa.md`](https://github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods/blob/develop/packages/client/DESIGN.pwa.md).

### The public website: editorial {#website}

The browser site is a coffee-table garden journal crossed with a living public record: a sticky,
transparent `SiteHeader`, Fraunces headlines at magazine sizes with Inter for everything else,
square corners on every button and card, generous photography, and editorial sections that stay
warm walnut in dark mode rather than cold grey. The home page and the four navigation pages
(Gardens, Impact, Fund, Actions) open on a three-line hero title at every width, and the accent
green is reserved for the install and support calls to action. Its rules live in
[`packages/client/DESIGN.browser.md`](https://github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods/blob/develop/packages/client/DESIGN.browser.md).

### The admin cockpit: Material 3, strictly {#admin}

Stewards and evaluators work in the cockpit, and its litmus test is blunt: if it would be out of
place in Linear, GitHub, or Stripe's dashboard, it is out of place here. The same linen canvas and
charcoal ink, but no serif, no lookbook layouts, and Plus Jakarta Sans throughout. Material Design 3
is the strict structural backbone, with exact component dimensions, M3 state layers, a six-step
shape scale, and one elevation ladder; spring motion is the only permitted deviation. The nav dock
(the `NavigationBar` and the FAB) is the only translucent surface, dialogs and side sheets and
dense content stay solid, and each workspace tints the canvas with its own tone. Its rules live in
[`packages/admin/DESIGN.md`](https://github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods/blob/develop/packages/admin/DESIGN.md).

### These docs {#docs}

A quiet reading space: Manrope body, Bricolage Grotesque headings, a plain Docusaurus shell, and
one primary green for active navigation everywhere. No cockpit chrome, no bottom navigation, no
hero treatment. Its rules live in
[`docs/DESIGN.md`](https://github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods/blob/develop/docs/DESIGN.md).

## How the system is governed {#governance}

DesignMD files own the visual rules, and they nest: start at the root
[`DESIGN.md`](https://github.com/greenpill-dev-guild/green-goods/blob/develop/DESIGN.md), then
read the DesignMD nearest the surface you're changing. Shared UI foundations live in
[shared](../packages/shared) with Storybook coverage, and the **live Storybook at
[design.greengoods.app](https://design.greengoods.app)** is the source of truth for what every
primitive looks like in both themes. Executable guards keep token usage and shell composition
honest in CI, so drift is caught by machines, not memory.

When you change UI: reuse a shared primitive before composing raw layouts, keep new strings in all
three locales, and check the matching Storybook story in the same pass.

## Seven regenerative principles {#seven-principles}

### Make the mycelium visible {#make-the-mycelium-visible}

Show how work, evidence, recognition, and funding connect. A user should be able to follow a value
flow without learning protocol internals.

### Design for succession {#design-for-succession}

Match complexity to the community's current capability. New gardens need clear starting points;
established gardens can carry denser coordination tools.

### Enrich the edges {#enrich-the-edges}

Give extra care to transitions between people and systems: gardener to steward, funder to garden,
online to offline, and familiar web behavior to on-chain records.

### Treat failure as a path forward {#failure-is-succession}

Recovery states should explain what happened, preserve work where possible, and make the next
useful action obvious. Rejection, disconnection, and delayed confirmation need context, not blame.

### Stay growth-agnostic {#be-growth-agnostic}

Measure verified regenerative outcomes and growing community capability, not compulsive
engagement. Seasonal rhythms replace artificial urgency, rankings, and fear-of-missing-out
patterns.

### Make capability the deliverable {#capability-is-the-deliverable}

The product should help communities do more for themselves. A useful test is whether the people
and records could remain legible if Green Goods were no longer the interface.

### Choose regen over degen {#regen-not-degen}

Avoid trading-terminal aesthetics, financialized status displays, and dark patterns. Favor
collective context, clear evidence, garden-level stories, and calm action.

## Source traditions {#sources}

These principles synthesize several traditions rather than claiming a new formal discipline:

- Ethan Roland and Gregory Landua's Eight Forms of Capital broaden how value is described beyond
  money.
- Kate Raworth's distributive and regenerative design framing asks systems to share value and
  operate within living limits.
- Elinor Ostrom's commons research informs local rules, transparent roles, monitoring, and nested
  governance.
- Suzanne Simard's work on forest networks and ecological research on succession and edge effects
  provide metaphors for visible flows, maturity, and boundary conditions.
- Regenesis, biomimicry, post-growth HCI, and Self-Determination Theory inform the emphasis on
  capability, context, autonomy, and non-extractive motivation.
- Solarpunk and Greenpill writing provide an optimistic visual and cultural frame for coordination
  around public goods.
