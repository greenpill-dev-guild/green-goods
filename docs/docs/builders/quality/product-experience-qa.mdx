# Product QA

Product QA asks the one question the test suite cannot: can a real person understand, complete,
recover from, and trust this flow? Passing builds prove the code runs; a QA walk proves the
experience works. Anyone on the team can take one. If you can open the app and describe honestly
what you saw, you are qualified.

## Where QA happens

Walks are recorded in the [QA app](https://qa.greengoods.app): a shared checklist where each tester
signs in with their wallet and owns their own verdicts. Every test case gets a Pass, Fail, Blocked,
or N/A call and a short note, saved as you go. Blocked means a required path cannot complete; N/A
means the case is outside the agreed scope. Each team pass is a **run**: the app records into the
open run, a tester starts a new run before a re-QA, and closed runs stay readable and comparable.

The app can follow the catalog by area (**Walk**), by urgency (**Priority**), or as a
cross-surface flow (**Journey**). [Test Cases](./test-cases) is the public catalog behind it,
including [how cases are added, changed, and retired](./test-cases#lifecycle). Findings land in
Linear after a session; screenshots and recordings go to the private Drive QA folder, and session
links are shared privately rather than published here.

:::tip First walk?
Take [`PUB-001`](./test-cases#public-website-p0), the simplest P0 on the sheet. Open
[greengoods.app](https://greengoods.app) in a desktop browser and visit `/`, `/gardens`, `/fund`,
`/impact`, `/actions`, and `/cookies`. Every page should show the website header and no app-style
bottom navigation. Record **Pass** with a one-line note, or **Fail** naming the route that looked
like the installed app instead. The walk itself needs no wallet or product role; recording the
verdict still needs your allowlisted wallet signed in to the QA app.
:::


## Walk a journey

However you enter a session, on a call, solo, or paired with an agent, the loop is the same:

1. Walk the real surface with the intended identity and environment.
2. Capture each observation before diagnosing it.
3. Reproduce visible defects from the rendered surface inward.
4. Fix only bounded, reversible work that fits the agreed session scope.
5. Revalidate in the same surface and record the remaining risk.
6. Hand deferred findings to QA triage (`qa-triage`, or the `qa-call-report` routine after a
   call), which files them in Linear as fix slices.

Journey mode is for two people who need to meet at the same handoffs. Both select the same
**Journey**; Tester A takes **Protocol & review** and Tester B takes **Garden & member**. **View**
picks whose recorded results you read, and **Part** picks which side of the workflow is yours.
Use two real, allowlisted wallets and keep the same identity from beginning to end: A is the
protocol steward and an independent steward or evaluator of the test Garden, never on a
contributor roster; B is a steward and member of that Garden, so B can claim for it and
contribute the Work.

| Phase | Tester A — Protocol & review | Tester B — Garden & member | Meet here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Record a starting assessment, then open the test Season and one Campaign. | Read the assessment and confirm the open cycle is visible. | Both people see the same Garden and cycle. The assessment adds context; it is not a prerequisite. |
| Protocol asks | Seed an approval-gated protocol Request, then accept B's Garden claim. | Open the Request and choose to take it up **for this Garden**. | The claim becomes Accepted. The Request stays in the protocol pool and names the Garden as provider. |
| Garden delivers | Review B's linked Work and evidence, approve it, then confirm fulfillment as the Request creator. | As accountable lead, submit Work and evidence against the original protocol Request. | Approved Work makes the Request ready, then Fulfilled. A stays outside the contributor roster. |
| Garden earns | Exercise the protocol-to-Garden earned payout step. | Verify the destination Garden Safe or its readiness state. | If settlement is unavailable, both record Blocked with the visible gate; nobody says value moved. |
| Garden distributes | Seed a separate commitment in the open Garden Campaign, then review and confirm B's delivery. | Take up the local commitment and submit its Work and evidence. | This is a second record, not a moved or mirrored protocol Request. |
| Member receives | Exercise the Garden-to-member contributor payout. | Verify the receiving account when the path is enabled. | If settlement is unavailable, record Blocked and keep the completed non-value workflow intact. |

Think of the service relay as **two records, one treasury relay**: the protocol Request stays in
the protocol pool while the Garden earns for delivering it, and the Garden then creates its own
commitment for the member who did the Work. A **Protocol treasury top-up** is different again:
discretionary funding with no commitment ID, never a stand-in for earned compensation.

At every handoff, the acting person pauses until the receiving person can see the new state. A
known gate is context, not a verdict: try the step, record Blocked only when the gate is present,
name what is missing, and continue with the remaining non-value steps.

## Run a team session

A team session is a scheduled call where two or more people walk different surfaces at the same
time, recording as they go. The QA app is the shared checklist; this is the checklist for the call.

**Before the call**

- Every tester's wallet is on the QA app allowlist and has signed in once. A five-minute smoke the
  day before saves twenty minutes of call time.
- The scope is picked: surfaces split and walked priority-first for a broad session, or one
  Journey with a Part per person for a shared flow. Where possible, people test work they did not
  build.
- One wallet identity and one product role per person for the whole Journey, and both people can
  see the test Garden before the first handoff.
- The write boundary is agreed: which funding and on-chain flows run end to end with real
  transactions, and which stop at the review step. The catalog's Transaction kind marks the cases
  this touches.
- If test cases changed since the last deploy, the QA app is redeployed first; a deployment pins
  the catalog revision it shipped with.

**During the call**

- Record a verdict and a short note per case as you walk. Screenshots and recordings go to the
  private Drive QA folder, never into public artifacts.
- In Journey mode, wait for the receiving person to observe each handoff before moving on. If
  settlement is Blocked, preserve that result and continue through the remaining non-value steps.
- Nobody takes minutes; the meeting notes capture the discussion. Spend the last half hour walking
  the merged fail list together and aligning on the cross-surface calls. Those decisions are the
  most valuable output of the call.

**After the call**

- Five minutes on four questions before everyone drops: what did we cover and consciously skip,
  what works well enough to leave alone, which fails worry us most, and would we welcome a new
  garden onto this build tomorrow?
- Trigger the `qa-call-report` routine, or run `/qa-triage --call` at a desk. It pulls every
  tester's verdicts from the call's run with `bun run qa pull`, keeps the entries from the call
  window, joins them with the call's notes by Test ID, and clusters failures into at most eight fix
  slices, each sized for one branch and one PR. `bun run qa report --slug <session>` derives the
  results-by-priority and results-by-kind blocks, so those numbers are never hand-counted.
- The routine enriches without blocking (the build under test, PostHog exceptions per surface,
  Sentry issues when that connector is wired), writes one `QA session YYYY-MM-DD` parent issue plus
  slice sub-issues to Linear, deduplicated against open QA issues and swept of tester attribution,
  wallets, and replay links, and posts a one-message summary to #product on Discord.
- Fix sessions work slice by slice, and a slice is done when its test cases pass again. Re-QA
  later in the week is a scoped walk of the affected cases in a new run, so the earlier pass stays
  intact and the compare shows what was fixed, what still fails, and what regressed. Within a run,
  the standing result is the most severe current verdict across testers.

## What every journey must prove

- The entrypoint, role, and selected Garden are clear; a wallet on the wrong network is told so
  and pointed to the fix.
- Loading, empty, disabled, error, offline, and recovery states are usable.
- A visible interaction changes state and provides legible feedback.
- Keyboard, focus, labels, touch targets, contrast, and reduced motion remain accessible.
- Saved-offline, pending-confirmation, and confirmed states look different and say which one
  you are in.
- Private identities, wallets, session data, and defect evidence stay out of public artifacts.
