## What is Green Goods?

**Green Goods helps communities document their regenerative work, get it verified, and get it funded.**

People who plant trees, clear waste, keep solar panels running, or teach a class record what they did in a phone app that works without a signal. Someone from their own community reviews it. The approved record is public, so the people who fund this kind of work can see it and choose to support it.

<Hero />

## What a garden is

A garden is a community doing regenerative work together in one place.

The word is a picture, not a job description. A garden is something a group of people tends over time, and it does not have to involve plants. A garden can be:

- a campus team keeping solar panels working,
- a neighborhood crew that collects and sorts waste,
- a collective restoring land with trees,
- a group running classes and workshops.

Every garden has its own people, its own list of the work it tracks, and its own funds. Green Goods currently covers four areas of work: **solar**, **agroforestry**, **education**, and **waste**.

## How it works, in one minute

1. **Plan.** The garden writes down where it is starting from and what it is aiming for.
2. **Document.** Gardeners record their work with photos and a few details, in the field.
3. **Review.** A steward from the garden approves each piece of work, or sends it back with a reason.
4. **Certify.** Approved work can be bundled into one claim, called an impact certificate. This step is still being switched on.
5. **Fund.** Supporters give to the garden, and the garden uses that support to do more work.

[How It Works](/community/how-it-works) walks through each step.

## What Green Goods is not

- **It is not only for gardening.** Any community doing regenerative work can be a garden.
- **It is not a payroll.** Approving work does not pay anyone. How a garden rewards its people is a separate step, and each garden decides it.
- **It is not an outside audit.** An approval means a steward from the garden looked at the evidence and stands behind it. It does not by itself measure environmental results.
- **It is not private.** What gardeners submit becomes a public, permanent record. That is the point, and it is worth knowing before you take the first photo.
