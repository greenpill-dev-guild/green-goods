# Where We're Headed

Green Goods helps a community turn care for a place into a credible, portable record that can coordinate people and attract durable support.

The story is one connected loop:

1. A **Garden** gives a community, its roles, and its records an accountable home.
2. An **Assessment** names the baseline, desired change, and evidence standard.
3. **Gardeners** submit **Work** as signed field records, including through the offline queue.
4. **Stewards** approve or reject that Work through a separate decision record.
5. Approved evidence can support portable impact claims and funding decisions.
6. Direct support, Cookie Jars, and Vaults help resources return to the work.

Evaluators strengthen Assessments, methods, and impact claims. They do not approve or reject submitted Work; that authority belongs to Stewards.

## How to read this direction

Green Goods separates product availability from protocol and design maturity:

- **Available** means people can use the product flow now.
- **Deployed, not available** means protocol capability exists onchain but its data and product surfaces are not ready.
- **In build** means implementation exists in part, with activation or integration still incomplete.
- **Planned** means the concept and intended behavior are specified but not implemented for use.
- **Vision** is a direction we are exploring, not a delivery promise.

The evidence-backed ledger is generated at [What Green Goods Can Honestly Claim](/community/green-goods-claims.generated). It is the safe source for partnership, grant, website, and campaign language.

## Available now

Today Green Goods can honestly say that communities can:

- organize roles and records around a shared Garden;
- define Actions and Assessments;
- capture Work in the field and sync it when connectivity returns;
- have Stewards record approval or rejection separately from the Work record;
- use direct operational support and longer-term Vault deposits.

These are the foundation: evidence capture, accountable review, and funding rooted in a real community rather than a platform-owned profile.

## Deployed protocol capability

Commitment Pooling is deployed on Arbitrum, unpaused, and has 18 registered pools. It can represent bounded Offers and Requests, cycles, recurring series, contributor rosters, evidence, confirmation, and non-transferable unit accounting.

It is **not yet an available Green Goods product flow**. The indexer, shared state/API, and client/admin surfaces remain blocked. Settlement contracts are deployed but paused, with value authority disabled. We should describe this as deployed protocol capability, never as a feature communities can already use.

## In build

The Impact Certificate path has implemented and deployed foundations, but activation and indexing remain environment-dependent. Safe language is: Green Goods is building the path that turns Assessment-grounded, Steward-approved Work into portable Impact Certificates.

We are also connecting the deployed coordination protocol to readable product surfaces. That work must preserve the same calm distinction between promises, evidence, recognition, and payment that the contracts enforce.

## Planned next

Community Needs & Signals begins the next part of the loop: communities name a **Need** and desired outcome before a solution is imposed. Signals, moderation, linked Commitments, and funding attribution can then build around it.

This layer is specified, not implemented. It should be described as planned until its contracts, joined reads, offline state, and Community/admin/funder surfaces are proven.

## Longer-term direction

The longer-term Green Goods picture is a community-owned coordination memory:

- evidence remains portable beyond any single interface;
- communities can name Needs and coordinate bounded commitments around them;
- recognition reflects verified contribution rather than activity volume;
- funding can support near-term operations and long-term capacity;
- conversational tools can lower reporting barriers without hiding authority or inventing proof;
- partner protocols can connect through explicit mappings instead of blurred claims.

WhatsApp/SMS reporting, broader cross-garden coordination, additional identity systems, and new funding rails remain vision or planned work until the capability ledger carries stronger evidence.

## The picture in one sentence

**Green Goods is the shared record and coordination layer that helps place-rooted communities show what they need, document what they do, verify decisions, recognize contribution, and bring resources back to the work.**
